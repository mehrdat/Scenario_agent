# Treatment — The Last Cheetah You've Never Heard Of

**Format:** 9:30 documentary essay · 16:9 · BBC narration + archive + camera-trap + Mehrdad outro
**Method:** Asif Kapadia archival-only structure for sections 1–6; Varda-style on-camera essayist for section 7.

---

## SECTION 1 — HOOK (0:00 – 1:00) · Opening Image · Misdirection → Reveal

**What we see.** Eight seconds of African cheetah: full sprint, golden grass, dust, a gazelle in the corner of the frame. The image the global audience has been trained to expect. Then a hard cut to dry Iranian plateau: ochre stone, sparse scrub, a single ridge of mountains in the far background. A still photograph of an Asiatic cheetah, lean and long-legged, standing on rock. Text card in restrained white serif: **یوز ایرانی — The Asiatic Cheetah**. A second card: **Fewer than 30 remain.**

**What we hear.** Narrator (BBC omniscient, female voice recommended for tonal contrast with Attenborough): *"Imagine a cheetah."* Pause two beats. *"You are imagining the wrong one."* A single low sustained string, no percussion. Wind. No music swell.

**Emotional beat.** Surprise → recognition → unease. The viewer has been gently caught.

**Pacing.** Fast first 8 seconds, then deliberate slowing. Three text cards, each held 3 seconds.

---

## SECTION 2 — WHAT MAKES THEM DIFFERENT (1:00 – 1:50) · Theme Stated

**What we see.** Side-by-side comparison plates: African cheetah skull / Asiatic skull; African coat / Asiatic coat (thicker, paler, longer winter fur); range maps now and a hundred years ago, the historical range bleeding from Arabia to India then shrinking like a closing iris to a single irregular patch in central Iran. One slow-motion camera-trap clip of an Asiatic male walking past the lens at night.

**What we hear.** Narrator names the subspecies in Latin once — *Acinonyx jubatus venaticus* — and never again. The Persian name **یوز (Yuz)** is given and will be used through the rest of the film. Ambient sound only; no score yet.

**Emotional beat.** Cognitive — the audience is being taught. Theme stated obliquely: *"This is a cat that the world forgot it had."*

**Pacing.** Steady, lecture-clean. This is the section that earns the right to slow down later.

---

## SECTION 3 — WHERE THEY LIVE (1:50 – 3:00) · Set-Up

**What we see.** Wide drone-style stills (sourced or AI-generated from real Touran reference imagery) of the four protected areas: **Touran, Miandasht, Naybandan, Kavir**. Each named in a lower-third. A single ground-level still of a dry riverbed. Camera-trap footage of a male Yuz drinking, three a.m., infrared green.

**What we hear.** Narrator describes elevation, temperature swings, prey species (jebeer gazelle, wild sheep, hare). First entry of score — sparse, sustained, single piano note motif that will return at Pirouz's death.

**Emotional beat.** Wonder, but cool — the audience falls in love with a landscape before they are asked to grieve it.

**Pacing.** Slowest section so far. Long holds on landscape stills, 6–8 seconds each.

---

## SECTION 4 — WHAT WENT WRONG (3:00 – 5:00) · Catalyst

**What we see.** Historical archive: black-and-white hunting photograph from the Qajar period; colour photograph of the last Indian cheetahs, 1947; news footage from the 1979 revolution (street scenes, not violence); Iran-Iraq war footage (long shots, dust, no faces); contemporary aerial of the Tehran–Mashhad highway slicing through arid terrain at sunset; one held still of a Yuz carcass at a roadside, framed wide, four seconds, no longer.

**What we hear.** Narrator delivers the historical chain plainly. No moralising. The score drops out entirely for the roadside still — only wind and the distant sound of a passing truck.

**Emotional beat.** Horror, but controlled. The audience is given enough to understand, never enough to look away from.

**Pacing.** Quickening through history, then a hard slow-down for the roadside image. The held silence is the punctuation.

---

## SECTION 5 — THE NAMED CHEETAHS (5:00 – 7:00) · B Story / Midpoint All-Is-Lost

**What we see.** Six portrait cards, one per cheetah, each held 8–10 seconds with a single photograph (research-sourced; see `raw/`) and a name card in Persian and English:

- **Kooshki** — کوشکی — male, captured young, founder of the captive programme
- **Delbar** — دلبر — female, "the one who took her time"
- **Iran** — ایران — female, named for her country
- **Koushki II** — کوشکی دوم — male, second-generation
- **Telma** — تلما — matriarch, the collared female whose data drew the map

Then a slow transition into the **Pirouz** sequence: a still of the newborn in a gloved human hand, dated **1 May 2022**. Brief Iranian state-TV-style graphics archive (lower thirds, news tickers). Children's drawings collected from social media. A clip of a vet weighing him.

**At ~6:30 — the All-Is-Lost beat.** A single still photograph of an empty veterinary incubator, lit by a fluorescent tube. Held for nine full seconds. Narrator, in a register half a step quieter: *"He was named Victorious. He lived ten months."* Cut to a final still of Pirouz alive, looking past camera. The piano motif from Section 3 returns, single notes, no accompaniment.

**Emotional beat.** This is the film's emotional centre. Investment → loss. The audience earns the right to grieve here because they have just spent two minutes learning to love six specific animals.

**Pacing.** Slowest sequence in the film. The empty incubator is the longest held shot in the cut.

---

## SECTION 6 — CONSERVATION TODAY (7:00 – 8:30) · Dark Night → Break into Three

**What we see.** Archive: news photographs of Dr **Kavous Seyed-Emami** (the conservation biologist who died in custody in 2018) — public photographs only, treated with restraint. A still of camera-trap equipment in a Pardisan Park laboratory. A map graphic showing the protected areas with overlay icons for: sanctions (red), conflict 2026 (orange), road kills (black). Camera-trap footage of an unidentified Yuz moving at dawn.

**What we hear.** Narrator names the three pressures — sanctions, the imprisonment and death of scientists, the 2026 regional war — without editorial. The score returns, slightly warmer, in low strings.

**Emotional beat.** Compounded grief, but a turn toward the next breath. The audience is being prepared to be asked something.

**Pacing.** Brisker than Section 5 — the film is moving toward its question.

---

## SECTION 7 — OUTRO (8:30 – 9:30) · Final Image · Mehrdad on camera

**What we see.** Cut to **Mehrdad**, mid-shot, 50mm, soft daylight from a window left, looking directly into the lens. No lower-third. He speaks. After about 30 seconds, cut away from him to the film's final piece of footage: **real camera-trap video of a wild female with five cubs** moving along a ridge at dusk, Touran, recent. The mother glances once back at her cubs. Cut to a final title card on black:

> **Kooshki · Delbar · Iran · Koushki · Telma · Pirouz (2022 — 2023)**
>
> **___________**
>
> Fewer than 30 remain. Five were born this year.

**What we hear.** Mehrdad's own voice, not professionally graded — slightly closer mic, room tone audible. Score absent until the wild-cubs footage, where a single sustained warm chord enters and holds through the title card. Last sound: wind on the ridge.

**Emotional beat.** Direct address → call → fragile hope. Not triumph. A question handed to the audience.

**Pacing.** Mehrdad's section is the only part of the film that breathes at conversational speed. The film ends on the longest held black of the cut.
