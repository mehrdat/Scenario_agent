## SECTION 2 — WHAT MAKES THEM DIFFERENT (1:00 – 1:50)

**[SCENE 08 · 1:00–1:18 · GRAPHIC + PHOTO split: African and Asiatic side by side]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
She is leaner than her African cousin.
Longer-legged. Paler. Her winter coat is thicker because her winters are colder.
Her Latin name is *Acinonyx jubatus venaticus*.
We will not use it again.

**[SCENE 09 · 1:18–1:30 · GRAPHIC: range map — historical range from Arabia to India]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
A century ago, this cat ran from the Arabian Peninsula to India.

**[SCENE 10 · 1:30–1:42 · GRAPHIC: range map retracts to central Iran]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
Today, every one of them is here.

## SECTION 4 — WHAT WENT WRONG (3:00 – 5:00)

**[SCENE 17 · 3:00–3:18 · PHOTO: Qajar-era hunting photograph, B&W]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
The killing began as a sport.

**[SCENE 18 · 3:18–3:30 · PHOTO: 1947 — the last Indian cheetahs, archival]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
In 1948, India lost hers.
Iran was the last country on Earth that still had this animal alive in the wild.

**[SCENE 19 · 3:30–3:48 · ARCHIVE: 1979 revolution street scenes, no violence]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)

For a year, no one watched the reserves.

**[SCENE 20 · 3:48–4:06 · ARCHIVE: Iran–Iraq war, long lens, dust]**

The deadliest predator of the Asiatic cheetah today is a truck at night.

## SECTION 5 — THE NAMED CHEETAHS (5:00 – 7:00)

**[SCENE 24 · 5:00–5:12 · PHOTO + NAME CARD: Kooshki / کوشکی]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
**Kooshki.**
A male, captured young. The founder of a programme that began with the question:
*Can we keep them alive ourselves?*

**[SCENE 25 · 5:12–5:24 · PHOTO + NAME CARD: Delbar / دلبر]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
**Delbar.**
The one who took her time.
For years, she would not breed.
And then, against every expectation, she did.

**[SCENE 26 · 5:24–5:36 · PHOTO + NAME CARD: Iran / ایران]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
**Iran.**
They named her for her country.

**[SCENE 27 · 5:36–6:00 · PHOTO + NAME CARDS: Koushki II and Telma]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
**Koushki the Second.** A male of the next generation.

And **Telma** — the matriarch.
A collar around her neck for two years drew the first true map of how a Yuz uses her land.
We learned more from Telma than from a century of hunters.

**[SCENE 28 · 6:00–6:18 · PHOTO + ARCHIVE: Pirouz, 1 May 2022, gloved hand]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
And on the first of May, in 2022 —
**Pirouz.**
*Victorious.*

The first Asiatic cheetah ever born in captivity.

**[SCENE 29 · 6:18–6:30 · PHOTO/ARCHIVE: Pirouz at three weeks, six weeks, three months]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
A country that had not had good news about its wildlife in a generation
followed his weight gain on the evening news.
Children drew him.
For three hundred days, he was a small, public miracle.

*(piano motif returns, single notes, alone)*

*(half a step quieter)*

## SECTION 6 — CONSERVATION TODAY (7:00 – 8:30)

**[SCENE 32 · 7:00–7:36 · PHOTO: Kavous Seyed-Emami + name card]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
The scientists who fight for her are running out of room.
**Kavous Seyed-Emami** — biologist, photographer, founder of the Persian Wildlife Heritage Foundation — was arrested in January 2018.
He died in custody two weeks later.

Other colleagues remain in prison.
Their crime was a camera in a tree.

**[SCENE 33 · 7:36–8:30 · GRAPHIC: map with three icon overlays + TRAP footage]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
Three forces close in.
Sanctions choke the veterinary supply.
The 2026 war pulls budgets and personnel north.
The road keeps killing.

*(score warms slightly — low strings)*

NARRATOR (V.O.)
And still — in a riverbed at dawn, in a place no one is watching —
she is still here.
