# Persian Cheetah — Final Documentary Script (v2, story-doctored)

> **Runtime:** ~9:06. **Format:** 16:9, 4K. **Mode:** narration-driven essay documentary, **no on-camera interviews** (Kapadia / Attenborough lineage). Archival stills and footage + AI-generated landscape plates + ICS camera-trap. **Language:** Farsi narration; English subtitle toggle. **Controlling idea:** *Extinction is not a sudden event; it is the sum of small, ignored choices — and to mourn the cheetah is to refuse those choices.*
>
> v2 changes vs. v1: see `danesh/behtar-log.md`. Headline: removed two on-camera interviews (Mohsen, Dr. Sara) to enforce the no-interview mode. Added Delbar. Tightened Section 5. All other beats preserved.

Seven sections.

---

## SECTION 1 — COLD OPEN: "THE LAST TWELVE" [0:00–0:58]

**Black. Wind.**

A single line, white serif on black, holds for three seconds:

> *On the central plateau of Iran, twelve cats are still breathing.*

Cut. Wide aerial of the Touran plateau at blue dawn. No music. Only wind and the distant call of a sandgrouse. Camera drifts low and slow over pale gravel, sparse silver-grey *Artemisia* tussocks, basalt outcrops black against the pre-sun sky. A snow-dusted ridge floats on the horizon.

**NARRATOR (V.O.) — calm, low:**
> Not on a savannah. Not in a jungle. Here. On a high, cold, sand-colored plain, two hundred kilometers from the nearest city.

Cut to camera-trap footage — grainy, infrared. A single cheetah crosses the frame at night. Eyeshine. A long tail. Then gone.

**NARRATOR:**
> This is the only place on Earth where the Asiatic cheetah still lives in the wild. Fewer of them than players on a football pitch.

Title card, austere serif, Farsi and English stacked:

> **یوزپلنگ ایرانی**
> *The Persian Cheetah*

Hold three seconds. Cut to black.

---

## SECTION 2 — WHAT IT IS, AND IS NOT [0:58–2:28]

A montage that *teaches the eye* to see the difference between this animal and the one the audience thinks they know.

Split-screen, briefly: African cheetah running through tall gold grass, left — Asiatic cheetah trotting across pale gravel, right. The right-hand image holds three beats longer than the left.

**NARRATOR:**
> When you say cheetah, most of us picture this. (*pause*) Tall grass. Acacia trees. A herd of zebra in the distance. (*beat*) Forget all of that.

Cut to a slow camera move across the gravel plain. Insert close-up: a single set of cheetah pawprints in sand.

**NARRATOR:**
> The Persian cheetah is a desert animal. Paler. Slightly smaller. A thicker ruff around the neck for the nights, which fall below freezing. Built not for the long chase, but for short bursts across rock and dust.

Archival illustration: a Sassanid silver plate, a king on horseback, a leashed cheetah at his side. Then a Safavid miniature — a "yuz" beside a noble. Slow pan over the artwork.

**NARRATOR:**
> A thousand years ago, our kings hunted with them. They had a name in our language all their own.
> *(full pause — three seconds of artwork and wind, no voice)*
> **Yuz.** Not leopard. Not lion. Yuz. From Arabia to India, this cat walked the dry lands of half a continent.

Map graphic — historical range bleeding red across Asia. Then, second by second, the red recedes. Arabia gone. Pakistan gone. India gone, with a date: **1947**. The red contracts until only a small patch in central Iran remains.

**NARRATOR:**
> The last Indian cheetah was shot in 1947. Today, this one patch of plateau is the entire world that holds them.

---

## SECTION 3 — THE LANDSCAPE [2:28–3:55]

A movement now — from the abstract to the specific. Name the places. Show them.

**On-screen lower-third:** *Touran Biosphere Reserve · Semnan Province.*

Wide drone shot, low, parallel to the ground, gliding across the steppe. Mountains in distance. A herd of jebeer gazelle trots through the foreground.

**NARRATOR:**
> Touran. Miandasht. Naybandan. Dareh Anjir. Names that mean nothing to most Iranians, and everything to twelve cats.

Cut to game-guard's-eye-level shot: a Land Cruiser dust trail moving along a dirt track. Cut to inside the vehicle — a silhouetted figure, binoculars up, profile against the windshield glare. We never see his face. We never hear his voice.

**NARRATOR:**
> A guard at Touran has worked these tracks for twenty-one years. In all that time, he has seen a cheetah, in person, with his own eyes — three times.

Cut back to plateau. Sun rising. Long shadows.

**NARRATOR:**
> The reserves are not zoos. They are vast — thousands of square kilometers of nothing, in which a few cats and their prey try to stay alive. One guard patrols an area the size of a small European country.

Slow tilt up from the gravel to the snow-capped ridge.

**NARRATOR:**
> The cheetah does not need much. It needs gazelle to hunt. It needs water — a spring, a seep, a frozen puddle in winter. It needs to not be hit by a truck.

Cut to a single static shot: an empty stretch of two-lane highway cutting across the plateau, asphalt shimmering. Hold five seconds. A truck blasts through frame.

## SECTION 4 — THE NAMES [3:55–5:55]

The emotional center. Four named animals carry the argument.

### KOOSHKI [3:55–4:33]

Archival footage of Kooshki at Pardisan Park, Tehran — pacing his enclosure, eyes the color of weak tea. The pacing is rectangular: eight steps, turn, eight steps, turn. The geometry of the captive.

**NARRATOR:**
> His name was Kooshki. He was a cub in Miandasht in 2007. Herders killed his mother near their flock. The Department of Environment found him alone and brought him to Tehran.

Photos: Kooshki as a young animal, Kooshki being weighed, Kooshki with biologists.

**NARRATOR:**
> He lived eleven more years in semi-captivity. He never hunted again. But he became — almost by accident — the face every Iranian child knew when they thought of a yuz. He died in 2018, of kidney failure, at twelve years old.

Beat of silence. Hold a still photograph of Kooshki, head turned to camera. Three seconds.

### DELBAR AND IRAN [4:33–5:15]

Cut to clinical archive: a feeding chart, a clipboard, two named lines.

**NARRATOR:**
> After Kooshki, the program continued. Two females at Touran — Delbar, and another named Iran — were the small bet that the species might be helped to breed itself back from twelve.

Archival from the Touran captive-breeding center, May 2022. A female cheetah, *Iran*, with three newborn cubs. The cubs are tiny, soaked, blind.

**NARRATOR:**
> In May of 2022, for the first time in recorded history, an Asiatic cheetah gave birth in captivity. Three cubs. The mother was Iran. The country, for a moment, allowed itself to hope.

Cut to grainy phone footage: the cubs at one week. Cut: the cubs at two weeks. A veterinarian's gloved hand holding a syringe.

**NARRATOR:**
> Two of the three died within fourteen days.

*Hold the syringe image. One full second of silence. The two deaths sit on screen.*

**NARRATOR:**
> The third — a male — survived. The Iranian Cheetah Society named him **Pirouz**. The Victorious.

### PIROUZ [5:15–5:55]

Photographs of Pirouz cycle through, slowly. As a four-week cub bottle-fed by a vet. As a three-month cub on a scale. As a six-month cub batting at a feather toy. The frame holds longer on each image. Music — a single sustained cello note — enters for the first time, low under the wind.

**NARRATOR:**
> For nine months, Pirouz was Iran's most-photographed citizen. School children drew him on Nowruz cards. Murals of him appeared on Tehran walls. He could not be released — he had imprinted on humans — but he was alive. And alive, just then, was enough.

Cut to news ticker, Farsi, scrolling: **۲۸ اسفند ۱۴۰۱ — یوزپلنگ پیروز درگذشت**.

**NARRATOR:**
> On the twenty-eighth of February, 2023, Pirouz's kidneys failed. He was flown by air ambulance to a specialist clinic in Tehran. He did not survive the procedure. He was ten months old.

Hold the news ticker. Then cut to a wall mural of Pirouz, a child standing beneath it. The child reaches up, fingertips just touching the painted paw.

**NARRATOR (quiet):**
> A nation grieved a cat. The cat, of course, knew nothing of it.

---

## SECTION 5 — WHY THEY ARE DYING [5:55–7:23]

Tone shifts — clearer, more reportorial. The film makes its case.

Animated infographic: five icons emerging on the gravel plain, one by one.

**NARRATOR:**
> They are dying for five reasons. We know each one. We have known each one for twenty years.

**1. ROADS.** Cut to dashcam footage of Highway 81 at night — the camera approaches a dark shape on the asphalt. Pull short of the impact frame; cut to a still photograph of the aftermath, censored to a silhouette.

> Roads. The Tehran–Mashhad highway and the Abbas-Abad corridor cut directly across cheetah range. Cats hit by trucks. Underpasses built — too few, too late.

**2. PREY.** Cut to a flock of sheep crossing what was, on the map, a wildlife corridor.

> The gazelle and wild sheep the cheetah hunts are themselves being hunted out, or crowded out by livestock.

**3. SHEPHERDS.** Cut to a long lens — a livestock-guard dog, a Sarabi, barking at the camera in middle distance.

> A cheetah caught near a flock is sometimes a cheetah killed by dogs or by a rifle. The mothers die. The cubs become orphans like Kooshki.

**4. FRAGMENTATION.** Aerial shot of a new mining road cutting across the plateau.

> Mines, new roads, ill-placed solar farms. Each is a small choice; together, a wall the cheetah cannot cross.

**5. ISOLATION.** Cut to a still: a packing crate of veterinary supplies at customs, stamped *HELD*.

> And — quietly, expensively — sanctions. The captive breeding program cannot easily import the equipment, the drugs, the genetic stock it needs. There is no second population to borrow from. There is no elsewhere.

Cut wide. A single cheetah, on the gravel plain, walks away from camera. Long lens. Heat shimmer.

---

## SECTION 6 — WHAT HAS NOT YET BEEN LOST [7:23–8:25]

The film breathes. It is not yet a eulogy.

Camera-trap footage, recent: a female cheetah with two cubs, moving along a wadi at night. The cubs play. The mother watches.

**NARRATOR:**
> And yet. In the autumn of 2023, a camera-trap in Touran caught this. A female. Two cubs. Not in a cage. Not in a clinic. On their own ground, walking.

Cut to a daytime drone shot — the same wadi by day, empty.

**NARRATOR:**
> The Iranian Cheetah Society now runs more than three hundred camera-traps across the reserves. Volunteers — many of them students — drive thousands of kilometers each season to swap memory cards. They are not paid. They go anyway.

Cut to a held title card — white serif on black, four seconds:

> *Every photograph is a small argument against despair. The animal is telling us — I am still here. Now what will you do?*
> — Field biologist, Iranian Cheetah Society

Cut to a classroom in a rural school. A teacher holding up a poster of a cheetah to a circle of children sitting on the floor. The children laugh. One imitates the cat — paws up, eyes wide.

**NARRATOR:**
> The animal does not know it is a symbol. The children do. And the children, in the end, are the only ones who will inherit what we choose now.

---

## SECTION 7 — CLOSING: THE CHOICE [8:25–9:06]

Return to the cold-open landscape. Blue dawn. Wind. The single cello note returns, slightly fuller.

**NARRATOR:**
> Twelve cats. Maybe fewer by the time you watch this. Maybe more — if, somewhere out past Naybandan, in a wadi a camera has not yet found, a female is hidden with her cubs.

Slow rise of camera — drone ascends, the plateau widening below.

**NARRATOR:**
> Extinction is not a single event. It is not a meteor. It is the sum of a thousand small, ignored decisions — to widen this road, to graze that valley, to defer the veterinary order one more month. None of those decisions, alone, kills a cheetah. All of them, together, do.

The drone reaches altitude. The plateau looks vast, indifferent, beautiful.

**NARRATOR:**
> The yuz was here when our kings hunted with him. He was here before there were kings. He may be here in fifty years — or he may not — and the difference between those two worlds is not luck. The difference is us.

Cut to a final camera-trap still: a single cheetah, head turned toward the lens, eyes glowing in the infrared.

Hold.

**NARRATOR:**
> *Twelve. Still breathing.*

Cut to black.

End-card, Farsi over English:

> **به یاد پیروز، ۱۴۰۱ — ۱۴۰۲**
> **In memory of Pirouz, 2022 — 2023.**
> **Iranian Cheetah Society · iraniancheetah.org**

Credits over wind. No music under credits.

## NOTES FOR THE EDIT

- **Mode:** essay documentary, no on-camera interviews. Anchors of credibility are: named places, named animals, dated events, one attributed pull-quote.
- **Pacing rule:** every section opens on landscape and closes on face — animal, silhouette, or child. The film's grammar is *place → person → animal → place*.
- **Music discipline:** no music until 5:15 (Pirouz). One cello note. It returns in Section 7. That is the entire score. The rest is wind, footsteps, vehicle distance.
- **No dramatic narration moves.** The narrator is informed, not inflamed. Trust the images.
- **The "us" rule:** the first-person plural appears in narration **exactly twice** — "*most of us*" in Section 2 (warm, conversational) and "*the difference is us*" in Section 7 (the address). No other "us" or "we." Hold the discipline in the mix.
- **Subtitle policy:** Farsi narration with toggleable English subs.
- **Length tolerance:** the cut may breathe to 9:45. Do not push past 10:30 — the argument loses force.
