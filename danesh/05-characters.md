# Character Bible — Persian Cheetah
> *Subjects, not characters.* This is a documentary essay film: the cheetahs are real, named animals. Their "arcs" are biographical, not dramaturgical. The bible's job is to make sure each animal lands as a **distinct emotional presence** on screen and is not lost in the species-level abstraction "the cheetah."

Source: `danesh/Persian-Cheetah-research.md`, `danesh/script.md`.
Style: Kapadia/Attenborough — no human interview characters foregrounded; the **animal is the protagonist of its own life.**

---

## 1. KOOSHKI (کوشکی) — *The Captive King*

- **Species role:** Adult male Asiatic cheetah, ex-situ (Pardisan Park, Tehran). Captured cub 2007, died 2018.
- **Visual silhouette:**
  - Slightly larger than expected for the subspecies. Coat slightly faded from indoor years — paler than wild kin.
  - Eyes "the color of weak tea" (script). Tear-stripes prominent.
  - Distinctive: a *pacing gait* — the eight-step rectangle of the captive cat. This is his shot. Whenever Kooshki is on screen and moving, he is pacing.
- **Silhouette test:** the only adult cheetah seen behind bars / mesh. Foreground geometry of enclosure = Kooshki.
- **Symbolic weight:** the **face of the species in Iranian memory**. Every Iranian over 20 knows him from school textbooks and IRIB nature segments. He is the audience's *introduction* to the yuz — the cat they think they remember.
- **Arc in the film:**
  - Section 4 only. Introduced as origin-story figure: the orphan cub, the herder rifle, the long captivity, the kidney failure death.
  - His function is to **install the pattern** — orphaning, captivity, kidney failure — that will repeat in his son Pirouz. The audience does not consciously notice. The grammar of the film does.
- **Emotional function:** *recognition + low-grade guilt.* The audience sees a familiar face and remembers that they used to care, and then stopped.
- **Audio signature:** silence. Kooshki is shown only in archival photographs and one paced-enclosure video. No vocalization. The cellos do not play for him.
- **The one shot:** the held photograph, head turned to camera, eyes meeting the lens. The film parks here for a beat of silence before moving to his mate.

  - Often photographed half-turned, mid-step, as if about to leave the frame.
- **Symbolic weight:** the *unfinished sentence*. Where Iran/Pirouz get news coverage, Delbar sits in the program file uncelebrated. She is the reminder that the program has more than one animal, that hope is technical and ongoing, not theatrical.

- **The one shot:** if archival exists — Delbar half-turned, eyes on the keeper, off-camera. Otherwise: a name on a clipboard, a feeding chart with her line of data.

## 3. IRAN (ایران) — *The First Mother*

- **Species role:** Adult female, captive-breeding program at Touran. **First Asiatic cheetah to give birth in captivity** — 1 May 2022, three cubs.
- **Visual silhouette:**
  - Lean, alert, *tired*. Post-partum thinness in the archive footage.
  - Always shown indoors — concrete pens, straw, fluorescent overhead.
  - The eyes are the giveaway: she watches the humans more than the cubs.
- **Silhouette test:** the only female cheetah shown *with cubs* in the entire film.
- **Symbolic weight:** the *nation as mother*. Her literal name — Iran — makes her the bearer of the national projection. The film does not over-press this. The naming is enough.
- **Arc in the film:**
  - Section 4.2 (4:40–5:20). She arrives, gives birth, loses two cubs in fourteen days, hands the survivor to a veterinarian. She does not appear again.
  - Her departure from the narrative is its own statement. She lived. The film moves on. So did the country.
- **Emotional function:** *the gap between effort and outcome.* She did the impossible thing. The impossible thing was not enough.
- **The one shot:** her face at week-two, watching the gloved hand take the surviving cub for bottle-feeding. No voiceover here. Hold three seconds.

## 4. THE TWO LOST CUBS — *The Unnamed*

- **Species role:** Two of Iran's three cubs. Died within fourteen days. Cause: one lung deformity, one unclear.
- **Visual silhouette:** wet, blind, palm-sized. Filmed once, briefly, in week one. Never named.
- **Symbolic weight:** the **non-event the film refuses to skip.** Most coverage of the 2022 birth jumped from "three cubs born" to "Pirouz, the survivor." The script names the two deaths in one sentence ("Two of the three died within fourteen days") — and the bible argues this sentence carries more weight than its length suggests. The story of an endangered species is *mostly* the story of failed cubs.
- **Arc in the film:** one line of narration. One beat of held image (the syringe in the gloved hand).
- **Emotional function:** *the casualty count the audience would otherwise forget.* The two losses prepare the emotional ground for the third loss (Pirouz) to land.
- **The one shot:** the gloved hand and syringe. No cub visible. The absence is the shot.

## 5. PIROUZ (پیروز) — *The Victorious*

- **Species role:** Surviving male cub of Iran's 2022 litter. Hand-raised. Imprinted on humans. Could not be released. **Died 28 February 2023 of kidney failure, at ten months.**
- **Visual silhouette across age stages — design these as a graded sequence:**
  - **Week 4:** palm-sized, eyes barely open, bottle-fed by a gloved hand. Vulnerable.
  - **Month 3:** kitten body, oversized paws, tear-stripes filling in. Wobbly.
  - **Month 6:** distinctly cheetah now — long-legged, batting a feather toy. *The image the country fell in love with.*
  - **Month 9:** lean, almost-adolescent, eyes weary. Last archival frames before the crisis.
  - **The aftermath:** never shown. The mural replaces the body.
- **Silhouette test:** only cub-aged cheetah shown solo to camera in the entire film. Wherever you see a cub's face filling the frame, it is Pirouz.
- **Symbolic weight:** the **child everyone watched grow up and bury.** A whole nation's grief was projected on this ten-month-old animal. The script handles this with restraint — one cello note, one news ticker, one mural, one child's hand. The bible insists: *do not add more.* The restraint is the meaning.
- **Arc in the film:**
  - Section 4.3 (5:20–6:00). Five photographs cycling through age stages. News ticker of death. Mural. Child reaches up.
  - Section 7 (end-card): "In memory of Pirouz, 2022–2023." His name closes the film.
- **Emotional function:** *the named loss.* The film argues — implicitly — that the audience cannot mourn "the species." They can only mourn a named animal. Pirouz is the named animal. To love him is to love what he was a member of.
- **Audio signature:** the **single sustained cello note** that enters at 5:20. It is *his*. It returns in Section 7 to bury him into the wider landscape.
- **The one shot:** the child's fingertip just touching the painted paw on the wall. Pirouz is, by this point, no longer in the film. He is only paint. The film knows this; the child does not.

## 6. THE UNNAMED CUB IN THE WADI — *The Possible*

- **Species role:** Anonymous wild cub, photographed (with mother and sibling) by an ICS camera-trap in Touran, autumn 2023. *Not in captivity. Not in a clinic. On their own ground.*
- **Visual silhouette:**
  - Infrared/black-and-white camera-trap footage. Grainy. Two small forms behind a watching adult.
  - Movement: *playful* — cubs batting at each other in the night. This is the only **playful** cheetah image in the entire film.
- **Silhouette test:** the only cheetah(s) shown **outside** human reach — no enclosure, no clinic, no road. The geometry of the shot is wadi and rock, not pen and gate.
- **Symbolic weight:** the **counter-argument.** Every named cheetah in the film is dead or captive. This anonymous cub is alive, free, and uncountable. The film's argument against despair sits on this one camera-trap clip.
- **Arc in the film:**
  - Section 6 (7:30–8:30). One sequence. Cubs playing, mother watching, then daytime drone of the empty wadi — to remind us that even the alive cubs are mostly absences on the land.
- **Emotional function:** *the door left open.* If the film ended at Pirouz, it would be a eulogy. The unnamed cub keeps the verdict pending.
- **The one shot:** the moment one cub jumps onto the other in infrared. Two seconds. Use it slow-motion if frame-rate permits without distortion.

## RELATIONSHIP MATRIX

| Pair | Relationship | What the audience reads |
|---|---|---|
| Kooshki → Pirouz | Father (genetic line; Kooshki was a breeder in the program before death) | Inherited fragility. Pattern of orphaning and kidney failure across generations. The film does not state this explicitly — the rhyme is in the editing. |
| Iran → Pirouz | Biological mother who could not nurse | The handover from animal mother to human keeper. The moment the species enters human dependency. |
| Iran → the two lost cubs | Biological mother of the unnamed dead | The 14-day silence. The price of the impossible birth. |
| Pirouz → the child at the mural | A dead cat and a living child | Inheritance. The film's whole argument compressed into one frame. |
| Pirouz → the unnamed wadi cub | Captive dead vs. wild alive | The two possible futures of the subspecies. The film refuses to choose. |
| Kooshki ↔ Iran ↔ Delbar | The captive triangle | The breeding program as a small, closed world. |

## ENSEMBLE EMOTIONAL DESIGN — what the named cats are *for*

The film names four animals so the audience has somewhere for grief to land. The trap: a documentary that names a single dead animal (Pirouz) and rides his death for the third act becomes manipulative. The script avoids this by:

1. **Frontloading Kooshki** — so Pirouz is not the first death, only the most recent.
2. **Naming the two unnamed cubs by their absence** — so the audience does not skip the casualty list.
3. **Refusing to name the wild cubs in Section 6** — anonymity is their safety; the film respects it.

The bible's instruction to the director: **do not add more named animals.** The math is correct.

## VOICE NOTE — NARRATOR AS THE FIFTH PRESENCE

The narrator is the only sustained human voice in the film. Treat them as an unseen character:
- **Register:** informed elder, not boom-voice authority. Reference: Farhad Mehrad gravitas, IRIB *Mostanad* restraint.
- **Posture toward the animals:** never anthropomorphizes. Never says "he was sad." Says the facts. Lets the audience supply the feeling.
- **Posture toward the audience:** assumes intelligence. Never explains the obvious. Never repeats a fact.
- **The shift:** at 8:30 the narrator's posture changes — from reporter to witness. "The difference is us." This is the only first-person plural in the script. Earn it by withholding it everywhere else.
