### Section 2 — What It Is, And Is Not [1:00–2:30]

### Section 3 — The Landscape [2:30–4:00]
**Diagnosis — STRUCTURAL ISSUE (the only real one).** The script includes an on-camera interview insert: *MOHSEN, DOE Game-guard.* The brief explicitly specifies **no-interview Kapadia/Attenborough mode.** The placeholder is a vestige of an earlier draft and breaks the documentary mode the film has chosen.
- **Prescription:** Remove the on-camera interview. Convert Mohsen from a speaking subject to a **silhouetted figure inside the Land Cruiser**, with the relevant biographical fact ("twenty-one years, three sightings") delivered by the narrator. This preserves the *information* and the *human cost* without breaking the no-interview rule. The guard becomes part of the landscape grammar, not a competing voice.
- **Why this matters:** the Kapadia method (cf. *Senna*, *Amy*) is precisely that *no one explains the subject to the audience.* The film *shows* and the narrator *contextualizes from above*. A live interviewee in the room collapses that distance and announces "this is now a journalism piece." It isn't. It is an essay.

### Section 4 — The Names [4:00–6:00]
**Diagnosis:** This is the script's emotional engine and it is well-built. Kooshki → Iran → Pirouz is the right order. The withholding of music until Pirouz (5:20) is disciplined and correct.
- **Minor: name Delbar.** Currently absent from the script though research lists her as the program's secondary breeding female (and possibly the dam in some accounts). One sentence between Kooshki and Iran handles this. The film's argument *the program continues past the news cycle* is materially stronger when a second living captive female is named.
- **Minor: the two lost cubs.** The line "Two of the three died within fourteen days" passes quickly. The bible argues this is *too quick* — the casualty count needs an extra half-beat. **Prescription:** add a one-second hold on the syringe-in-gloved-hand image, no voiceover. Silence carries the deaths.
- **Pirouz beat — no change.** The single cello note entering on Pirouz is the script's most disciplined choice. Protect it.

A pull-quote in white-on-black, held for four seconds, preserves the credibility and the discipline.

## CONTROLLING-IDEA AUDIT

> *"Extinction is not a sudden event; it is the sum of small, ignored choices — and to mourn the cheetah is to refuse those choices."*

Each section's relationship to the idea:
1. **Cold open** — *the consequence* (twelve cats).
2. **What it is** — *establishing the subject of the choices* (so the audience knows what they are choosing for or against).
3. **The landscape** — *the place where the choices happen* (highways, mines, livestock).
4. **The names** — *the cost of choices already made* (Kooshki, Iran's cubs, Pirouz).
5. **Why they are dying** — *the choices themselves, listed* (five named pressures).
6. **What has not yet been lost** — *the choices not yet made* (the wild cubs, the children).
7. **The choice** — *the address to the audience* ("the difference is us").

The idea threads through every section. Verdict: **the film knows what it is about.**
