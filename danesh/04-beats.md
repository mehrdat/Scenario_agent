# Beat Sheet — The Last Cheetah You've Never Heard Of

**Model:** Save-the-Cat adapted for 9:30 documentary essay. Page references replaced with timecode.

---

## 1 · Opening Image (0:00 – 0:08)
African cheetah at full sprint across golden savanna. Dust, gazelle, the postcard image the audience has been trained to expect. This is the film's *false* opening — the world the audience thinks they're in. Its function is to be overturned. The final image (cubs on an Iranian ridge) will mirror and answer it.

## 2 · Theme Stated (0:30 – 1:00, again at 1:00 – 1:50)
Spoken twice, obliquely. First as the misdirection closes: *"You are imagining the wrong one."* Then in Section 2 as the comparative biology sequence lands: *"This is a cat the world forgot it had."* The film's argument: **a species disappears when it becomes anonymous; naming refuses erasure.**

## 3 · Set-Up (1:50 – 3:00)
The world the Yuz lives in. Four protected areas named. Elevation, climate, prey. Camera-trap footage of a single male establishes that the species is real, alive, *present*. The audience is taught the geography before they are asked to grieve its damage. The piano motif is planted here — to be paid off at Pirouz's death.

## 4 · Catalyst (3:00 – 5:00)
What went wrong, in a chain: Qajar hunting → Indian extinction 1947 → 1979 revolution disruption → Iran-Iraq war → road infrastructure → vehicle collision as the dominant cause of death today. The roadside-carcass still is the inciting incident of the audience's emotional involvement. After this shot, the film is no longer descriptive — it is a story with stakes.

## 5 · B Story (5:00 – 6:00)
The six named cheetahs introduced as portraits: Kooshki, Delbar, Iran, Koushki, Telma. The B story carries the theme — *every one of these animals has a name, and naming is what saves them*. This section also seeds the audience with affection that will be cashed in at the midpoint.

## 6 · Fun & Games / Promise of the Premise (6:00 – 6:30)
Pirouz's birth, May 2022. The premise the film opened with — *we can keep these animals alive* — performed in full. Pirouz on Iranian state TV. Children's drawings. Vet's gloved hand. The audience is allowed to feel hope. This is the only sustained warmth in the film.

## 7 · Midpoint / All-Is-Lost (~6:30)
**Pirouz dies of kidney failure at ten months.** Empty incubator, held nine seconds. Narrator: *"He was named Victorious."* This is the film's load-bearing emotional beat. Structurally, it is *both* midpoint reversal *and* all-is-lost — compressed because the runtime is 9:30, not 110 minutes. The audience has been led specifically here for this loss.

## 8 · Dark Night of the Soul (7:00 – 8:30)
Sanctions. Kavous Seyed-Emami's death in custody, 2018. Other scientists imprisoned. The 2026 regional war diverts budget and attention. The conservation programme has lost: a cub, its scientists, its funding, and the world's attention. This is the film's lowest point. The audience is held in it for a full ninety seconds — long enough to feel it, short enough not to lose them.

## 9 · Break into Three / Final Image (8:30 – 9:30)
The narrator falls silent. **Mehrdad** appears on camera — the only human face in the film — and delivers the film's call: *if you do not know her name, she will go quietly.* Cut to real camera-trap footage of a **wild female with five cubs** on a Touran ridge. The final title card lists the six names and leaves a seventh line blank. The opening image of an African cheetah on golden grass is answered by an Iranian mother and five cubs on grey stone. The argument completes: naming is the act that keeps the species alive.

---

## Controlling Idea (proof)

> A species does not disappear in silence; it disappears in anonymity.

Every beat above either *names* (Section 5, Pirouz, the scientists, the title card) or *withholds a name* (the African cheetah of the opening; the unnamed wild mother of the closing). The argument is structural, not rhetorical.
