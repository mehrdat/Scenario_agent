# Naghd — Critique of `script-v2.md`
> Patient: *Persian Cheetah*, essay documentary, ~9:06, no on-camera interviews.
> Critic posture: a story editor reading the script aloud once, marking what worked, what nearly worked, and what didn't.
> Mode of critique: diagnostic only — *no rewrites* in this file. Rewrites are logged separately in `behtar-log.md`.

---

## VERDICT IN ONE LINE

A disciplined, well-built essay documentary that knows what it is about and trusts the audience to keep up. Strongest in its grammar (place → person → animal → place), weakest at one or two transition seams. Almost no dead weight. **9 / 10.**

---

## 1. STRUCTURE — DOES THE 7-SECTION SPINE HOLD?

**Yes.** The seven-section spine is the script's foundation and it is sound. Each section has:

- a **local question** the section answers (Sec 1: where? Sec 2: what is this animal? Sec 3: where, specifically? Sec 4: who has died? Sec 5: why? Sec 6: is there hope? Sec 7: whose call is it?),
- a **value-charge shift** (Sec 1 neutral → 4 negative → 6 cautiously positive → 7 morally engaged),
- a **clear cut point** (each section ends on a landed image: ridge tilt, range map, truck, mural, walking-away cheetah, classroom, eyeshine).

The film's grammar — *place → person → animal → place* — holds across every section. This is a Kapadia/Attenborough discipline and the script never breaks it.

**Strength of the spine in numbers:**
- 7 sections in 9:06 → avg. 1:18 per section. Tight, never indulgent.
- 5 named animals across the script (Kooshki, Delbar, Iran, Pirouz, the unnamed wild cubs). Four are dead or captive; one is free. The structural ratio is the argument.

**Where the spine bends slightly:**

- The **Sec 6 → Sec 7 transition** (8:25 hand-off) is the only seam I'd flag. Section 6 closes on children laughing in a classroom — a warm note. Section 7 opens by returning to the cold-open landscape and the cello — a cold note. The cut is right thematically (warm domestic → cold universal) but it asks a lot of the editor. Recommend a 2-second wind-only beat between the classroom and the drone-ascent to let the audience cross. Not a script change — an editorial note.

- The **Sec 4 → Sec 5 transition** (5:55) is also a hard pivot — from the mural/child silence into the reportorial five-reasons mode. The narrator handles it with "They are dying for five reasons. We know each one." That single line carries a lot of weight; the mix has to give it space.

**Risk you avoided:**

- The script does **not** front-load Pirouz. Many drafts of a story like this would open with the dead celebrity-cub, milk the grief, and then back-fill the context. Yours holds Pirouz until 5:15 and earns him through Kooshki and the two lost cubs. This is the most important structural choice in the film and it is correct.

## 2. CHARACTER / SUBJECT — DO THE NAMED CHEETAHS LAND?

**Mostly yes. One reservation.**

- **Kooshki** lands. The captive-king pacing image, the orphan origin story, the kidney-failure death in 2018 — by the time we reach Pirouz, the audience has been quietly trained to recognize the *pattern* (orphaned cub → captivity → kidney failure). The script never says it. The editing rhymes it. Good craft.

- **Iran** lands, but barely. She gets two minutes of screen time — birth, two deaths, hand-off of Pirouz to the vet — and then she is gone from the film. This is deliberate (see character bible) but the audience may not register her as a named *individual*; they may register her only as "Pirouz's mother." Whether that's a flaw depends on what you want her to do. The film's argument is *not* about her, so the limited landing is acceptable.

- **Delbar** is the v2 addition. She gets one sentence: *"Two females at Touran — Delbar, and another named Iran — were the small bet."* She is named, then never appears again. This is a calculated risk. The bible argues that naming her is structurally important (the program continues). The critic agrees but flags: one sentence is the floor. If you want her to land emotionally, she needs at least one image. Recommend the clinical feeding-chart insert specifically show her name in archival on screen. The sentence + the chart = enough.

- **The two unnamed dead cubs** land *by absence*. The one-second silent hold on the syringe (a v2 addition) is the right beat. The film refuses to skip them.

- **Pirouz** lands hardest. He had to. The script handles him with restraint — five photographs, one cello note, one news ticker, one mural, one child. **The restraint is the meaning.** A less disciplined film would have added another two minutes of news clips, social media reactions, mourners, etc. Yours doesn't. Trust the audience.

- **The unnamed wild cubs (Sec 6)** land as the film's structural counter-argument. One IR camera-trap clip + one daytime drone of the empty wadi. The anonymity is the point: the named animals are dead, the unnamed are alive. *The animal is telling us — I am still here.* Strong.

**Reservation:** the audience exits the film able to name two cheetahs cleanly (Kooshki, Pirouz). Iran is "Pirouz's mother." Delbar is a name. The wild cubs are deliberately unnamed. **This is fine.** Five clean character recalls would be too many for a 9-minute essay film. Two and a half is correct.

## 3. NARRATION VOICE — THE BBC → PERSONAL OUTRO SHIFT

The script asks the narrator to be informed-but-not-inflamed for 8 minutes, then to break into the first person plural in the last 40 seconds: *"The difference is us."* This is the most ambitious move in the script and it almost fully works.

**What works:**

- The cold-open narration ("Not on a savannah. Not in a jungle. Here.") immediately establishes the register: *correcting the audience's assumption.* Not lecturing. Correcting. This is the right posture for the next 8 minutes.

- The narrator never editorializes. Even at the most emotional moments — Pirouz's death, the syringe — the narrator gives only facts. "He did not survive the procedure. He was ten months old." This is the Attenborough lineage at its best.

- The shift to first-person plural at 8:25 ("The difference is us") is **earned** because the script holds the discipline everywhere else. There is no other "us/we" in the script except a single conversational "most of us" in Section 2 ("When you say cheetah, most of us picture this"), which is warm and harmless.

**What is borderline:**

- The narrator's one line at the end of Section 4 — *"A nation grieved a cat. The cat, of course, knew nothing of it."* — is the script's single most editorial sentence. It is the closest the narrator comes to commentary. It is also one of the script's best lines. Whether it crosses a line depends on the voice cast in the recording booth: read with even the slightest knowing tone, it is grandstanding. Read flatly, like a fact, it is devastating. Brief the narrator carefully.

- The final line, *"Twelve. Still breathing."* — *italicized in the script* — is brave. It risks coming off as poetic flourish. It works only because the rest of the film has been spare. Hold the discipline in the mix.

**Risk:**

- If the Farsi narration is delivered with even a hint of *Mostanad*-channel boom-voice or actorly gravitas, the entire script collapses into the very documentary mode it is trying not to be. Cast a calm, slightly tired, intelligent voice. Reference: late Farhad Mehrad register, not contemporary newscaster.

## 4. CINEMATOGRAPHY GUIDANCE — IS IT SPECIFIC ENOUGH?

**Yes — this is the script's quietest strength.**

The script names places (Touran, Miandasht, Naybandan, Dareh Anjir), substrates (gravel, *Artemisia*), light conditions (blue dawn, hard white midday, heat shimmer), specific lenses by implication (the long-lens cheetah walk-away in Sec 5; the dolly-style camera moves in Sec 1), and even diegetic-sound choices (truck shatters silence; classroom imitation = laugh). A cinematographer can read this script and know what to shoot.

**Where the visual notes are strongest:**

- Sec 1's *"snow-dusted ridge floats on the horizon"* — single phrase, full visual.
- Sec 5's *"asphalt shimmering. Hold five seconds. A truck blasts through frame."* — the timing is in the script. The editor inherits it.
- Sec 4's pacing-rectangle Kooshki shot — the geometry of captivity in one image.

**Where the visual notes could be sharper:**

- Sec 3's silhouetted game-guard is described as *"a silhouetted figure, binoculars up, profile against the windshield glare."* This is good but could be more specific: is the silhouette in the driver's seat or the passenger seat? Which direction is the binoculars facing relative to camera? Small thing — the kind of clarification that saves a half-day on set.

- Sec 6's classroom doesn't specify *where* in Iran. Is this a Khorasan village school? A Kerman one? A Touran-adjacent one? Specifying changes the costume, the kilim, the wall colour. Recommend: pick one. Even saying "a one-room school in Sabzevar" gives the location scout everything.

- Sec 4's *Pirouz photo montage*: the script lists three photos (4 wk, 3 mo, 6 mo). The character bible recommends a fourth at 9 mo (last archival before crisis) and the v2 script implies but does not name it. Add the explicit list of four ages so the editor doesn't have to negotiate.

**Visual discipline elsewhere:**

- The colour palette is implied but never stated. The research note (§2) names it: bone-white, ochre, terracotta, dusty blue mountain, charcoal basalt, single accent of cheetah cinnamon-sand. Recommend importing this into the script's "Notes for the Edit" section so the colourist inherits it.

## 5. CONTROLLING-IDEA ALIGNMENT

> *Extinction is not a sudden event; it is the sum of small, ignored choices — and to mourn the cheetah is to refuse those choices.*

Every section serves this idea (see `behtar-log.md` for the section-by-section trace). The idea is stated explicitly only once, at 8:30: *"Extinction is not a single event. It is not a meteor. It is the sum of a thousand small, ignored decisions…"* This is the right place for it. Earlier statement would be didactic. Later would be too late.

The film is **about something**, and it knows it. Most documentary essays fail this test. Yours doesn't.

---

## 6. DOCUMENTARY MODE CONSISTENCY

**Fixed in v2.** The original script broke its no-interview mode in Sections 3 and 6 with on-camera placeholders for Mohsen (game-guard) and Dr. Sara (biologist). The v2 removed both — replacing the game-guard with a silhouette + narrator paraphrase, and the biologist with a white-on-black pull-quote card. The Kapadia / Attenborough lineage is now consistent end-to-end.

**One thing to verify in production:** the pull-quote at Sec 6 is attributed only to "Field biologist, Iranian Cheetah Society." This is a documentary credibility move and it needs a real source. Either get the quote on the record from a real ICS biologist (preferable), or paraphrase from existing public statements with on-screen attribution and a date. Do not fabricate.

## 8. WHAT THE FILM IS *NOT* DOING (and could)

These are not flaws. They are deliberate exclusions. Worth naming so the choices are conscious:

- **No livestock-herder voice.** The film names herders as one of the five pressures (Sec 5.3) but never humanizes them. A film of double the length could; this one cannot, without breaking the structural pace. Acceptable.
- **No politician / government voice.** Mentioned implicitly (DOE, the bureaucratic delay) but never given a face. Acceptable for a 9-minute essay.
- **No African comparison footage beyond the Sec 2 split-screen.** This is restraint. A different film could spend three minutes on the African / Asiatic genetic split. This one doesn't. Acceptable.
- **No call-to-action button.** The end-card names the Iranian Cheetah Society and the URL. There is no "donate now," no on-screen petition. This is the right choice for an essay film. The argument is moral, not transactional.

## 9. ONE-LINE NOTES FOR THE EDITOR

- Hold the cello-note discipline. The score is *one note*, twice.
- Do not score the credits. Wind only.
- Do not let the news-ticker red strip linger more than 3 seconds. The audience reads it once.
- The syringe hold (Sec 4.2) is silent. One second. Resist the temptation to cut sooner.
- The fingertip-to-paw frame is the cut. Resist the temptation to hold longer.

---

## 10. OVERALL VERDICT

This is a documentary essay that has done its homework — about its subject, about its form, about its own discipline. It refuses to be a eulogy and refuses to be a polemic. It earns its single moment of address ("the difference is us") by holding back everywhere else. The Pirouz beat is the kind of restrained grief that most contemporary documentary cannot bring itself to make.

The strongest contribution v2 made was enforcing the no-interview mode the script had been claiming but not practicing. With the interviews removed, the film's grammar is fully coherent: a single calm narrator, the animals, the places, the named human absences (the game-guard silhouette), and one attributed pull-quote.

**Score: 9 / 10.**

**The one point withheld:** the Sec 6 → Sec 7 transition asks the editor for a small mercy that the script could provide on the page. A two-second wind-only beat written into the script as `*[wind. two seconds. then drone ascent.]*` would make the transition unmissable. This is a trivial fix and I have logged it in `behtar-log.md` for the next pass.

Otherwise — this is a script that can be shot.
