## SECTION-LEVEL DIAGNOSIS & CHANGES

### Section 1 — Cold Open [0:00–1:00]
**Diagnosis:** Excellent in the draft. The misdirection (Africa → Iran), the three title cards, the wind-only silence — all preserved.

**Changes applied:**
1. **Added a held beat before the word "Yuz."** The draft delivered "In Persian, her name is Yuz" as a single line. Polished version: narrator pauses; *"Yuz"* is its own line. *Why:* the word is the section. The pause forces the audience to receive the name, not hear it.

### Section 2 — What Makes Them Different [1:00–1:50]
**Diagnosis:** Strong didactic montage. Split-screen comparison is the right call. The Latin name said once, then dropped, is disciplined.

**Changes applied:**
1. **Tightened the India-1947 line.** The draft had this fact split awkwardly across two scenes (Scene 18 in Section 4 also said "In 1948, India lost hers"). The polished version corrects the date to **1947** consistently (per research note) and lets the map-graphic in Scene 10 carry the historical compression. The Section 4 retelling now reinforces, not duplicates.

### Section 3 — Where They Live [1:50–3:00]
**Diagnosis — STRUCTURAL ISSUE (the only real one in the draft).** The draft contained an on-camera interview placeholder (*MOHSEN, DOE Game-guard*) that broke the no-interview Kapadia mode the film has chosen.

**Changes applied:**
1. **Removed Mohsen interview. Replaced with silhouette + narrator line.** New Scene 15a: silhouetted DOE game-guard inside a Land Cruiser, profile against windshield glare. Narrator: *"A game-guard has worked this reserve for twenty-one years. He has seen three."* The information is preserved. The interview format is not.
   **Why this matters:** the Kapadia method (cf. *Senna*, *Amy*) is precisely that *no one explains the subject to the audience.* The film *shows* and the narrator *contextualises from above.* A live interviewee in the room collapses that distance and announces "this is now a journalism piece." It isn't. It is an essay.

### Section 4 — What Went Wrong [3:00–5:00]
**Diagnosis:** Strong. The historical chain (Sassanid hunt → Qajar photo → 1947 India → 1979 revolution → war → road) is well-paced.

**Changes applied:**
1. **Added Scene 17 — Sassanid/Safavid hunting miniature.** The draft jumped straight to the Qajar photograph; the polished version opens Section 4 with the royal-yuz miniature and a new line: *"For two thousand years, kings rode beside her. They called her Yuz — and they trained her to hunt with them."* This change earns three things at once: (a) the cultural depth required for the international audience to understand the *Yuz* word's weight, (b) a longer historical runway before the violence begins, (c) an Iranian-pride beat early in the wound section to balance the grief that follows.

### Section 5 — The Named Cheetahs [5:00–7:00]
**Diagnosis:** This is the script's emotional engine and it was well-built. The order Kooshki → (gap) → Iran → Pirouz worked.

**Changes applied:**
1. **Added Delbar as a full portrait beat.** The draft skipped from Kooshki to Iran without naming Delbar — though research lists her as the program's secondary breeding female. The polished version inserts Scene 25 (Delbar, 5:12–5:24): one sentence, one photograph, one name card. *Why:* the film's argument *the program continues past the news cycle* is materially stronger when a second living captive female is named. It also corrects an asymmetry where the script gave the dead animals most of the attention.
2. **Promoted Koushki II and Telma to full portrait beats.** The draft combined them into Scene 27 ("Koushki the Second… And Telma — the matriarch…"). The polished version splits this into Scene 27a (Koushki II, 5:36–5:48) and Scene 27b (Telma, 5:48–6:00). *Why:* the film's grammar is *one name per portrait card*. Telma in particular deserves her own beat — she is the only named cheetah in the film whose contribution is *data, not body*.
3. **Added Kooshki's death cause.** The draft introduced Kooshki without saying he died of kidney failure in 2018. The polished version adds this — *"He died in 2018. Kidney failure."* — at the end of Scene 24. *Why:* the rhyme with Pirouz's death (kidney failure, generations apart) is one of the film's most powerful structural choices. Stating Kooshki's cause earns the rhyme.
4. **Added a one-second silent hold on the syringe image for the two lost cubs.** New Scene 28a (6:12–6:18): the gloved hand with syringe, no cub visible, held silent. *Why:* the line "Two of the three did not see fourteen days" was passing too quickly. The bible argues — correctly — that the casualty count needs an extra half-beat. The silence carries the deaths.
5. **Pirouz beat — no change to musical timing.** The single cello note entering at 6:30 is the script's most disciplined choice. Protected.
6. **Pirouz line softened.** The draft said *"He was named Victorious. He lived ten months."* — unchanged. But the parenthetical direction tightened from *"(half a step quieter)"* to be more specific: narrator drops register and slows pace simultaneously.

### Section 6 — Conservation Today [7:00–8:30]
**Diagnosis — SECOND STRUCTURAL ISSUE in the draft.** The draft contained a *Dr. Sara* interview insert (or its placeholder) that, like Mohsen, broke the no-interview mode. The Seyed-Emami photo + narrator line was strong; the pivot to the three forces (sanctions / war / road) was tight; but the section ended without a hinge to Section 7.

**Changes applied:**
1. **Removed Dr. Sara interview. Replaced with on-screen pull-quote.** New Scene 33b: a white-on-black pull-quote card, *"Every camera we put in a tree is an act of patience." — Iranian Cheetah Society biologist.* Four seconds. *Why:* the line is doing important *thematic* work — it states the film's argument-against-despair. Keeping the *content* and dropping the *delivery format* is the move. A pull-quote preserves the credibility and the discipline.
2. **Added the bridging hinge line.** The draft ended Section 6 abruptly. The polished version adds (Scenes 33c–33d): camera-trap footage of an unidentified Yuz at dawn, then daylight drone of empty wadi, under the narrator: *"And still — in a riverbed at dawn, in a place no one is watching — she is still here."* This line is the structural hinge into Section 7. *Why:* without it, the film falls off a cliff between despair and Mehrdad's arrival. The hinge gives the audience permission to breathe.

### Section 7 — Outro [8:30–9:30]
**Diagnosis:** Excellent in the draft. The Mehrdad address + the wild mother with five cubs + the final title card with six names + a blank seventh line — all preserved.

**Changes applied:**
1. **Verified the "we / us" discipline.** The bible asks: no first-person plural anywhere before Mehrdad. Audit complete. The polished script uses *we* only:
   - Scene 08: *"We will not use it again."* (regarding the Latin name) — narrator referring to the film itself, not to a national "we." Acceptable.
   - Scene 27b: *"We learned more from Telma…"* — narrator's "we" refers to scientific knowledge. **Watch this in mix.** If it reads as the same "we" as Mehrdad's, soften to *"More was learned from Telma…"* in pickup. Currently judged acceptable because Mehrdad's "we" is paired with direct address to camera; this earlier "we" is omniscient and abstract.
   - Mehrdad's two uses in Scene 34a: *"I made this because I am Iranian. And because almost no one outside Iran knows we have a cheetah."* The "we" here is the Iranian national "we," not the film's "we." This is the earned moment.
2. **Final title card unchanged.** The blank seventh line is the film's argument in punctuation. Untouched.
