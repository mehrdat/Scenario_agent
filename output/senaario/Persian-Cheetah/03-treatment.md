# Treatment — The Last Cheetah You've Never Heard Of
## یوز — آخرین‌ها

**Format:** 9:30 documentary essay · 16:9 · BBC narration + archive + camera-trap + Mehrdad outro
**Method:** Asif Kapadia archival-only structure for sections 1–6; Varda-style on-camera essayist for section 7.
**Visual rule:** every section opens on landscape and closes on a face (animal, silhouette, or child).

---

## SECTION 1 — HOOK (0:00 – 1:00) · Opening Image · Misdirection → Reveal

**What we see.** Eight seconds of African cheetah at full sprint: golden grass, dust, a gazelle in the corner of the frame. The image the global audience has been trained to expect. Then a hard cut to dry Iranian plateau: ochre stone, sparse scrub, a single ridge of mountains in the far background. A still photograph of an Asiatic cheetah, lean and long-legged, standing on rock. Text card in restrained white serif: **یوز ایرانی — The Asiatic Cheetah**. A second card: **Fewer than 30 remain.** A third card, head-turned-away portrait. Silence.

**What we hear.** Narrator (BBC omniscient, female, Attenborough cadence): *"Imagine a cheetah."* Pause two beats. *"You are imagining the wrong one."* A single low sustained string under wind. No percussion. No score swell.

**Emotional beat.** Surprise → recognition → unease. The viewer has been gently caught.

**Pacing.** Fast first 8 seconds, then deliberate slowing. Three text cards, each held 3 seconds. The section ends in wind-only silence.

**Section grammar:** opens on running motion (Africa), closes on stillness (Iranian portrait).

---

## SECTION 2 — WHAT MAKES THEM DIFFERENT (1:00 – 1:50) · Theme Stated

**What we see.** Side-by-side comparison plates: African cheetah / Asiatic cheetah (the right panel: paler, leaner, thicker neck ruff). Range maps now and a hundred years ago — the historical range bleeding from the Arabian Peninsula to India, then shrinking like a closing iris to a single irregular patch in central Iran. One slow-motion camera-trap clip of an Asiatic male walking past the lens at night, infrared.

**What we hear.** Narrator names the subspecies in Latin once — *Acinonyx jubatus venaticus* — and announces it will not be used again. The Persian name **Yuz (یوز)** is given full pause — *the word is the section*. Ambient sound only; no score yet. The piano motif is held in reserve for Section 3.

**Emotional beat.** Cognitive — the audience is being taught. Theme stated obliquely: *"This is a cat that the world forgot it had."*

**Pacing.** Steady, lecture-clean. This is the section that earns the right to slow down later.

**Section grammar:** opens on graphic comparison (cold information), closes on a single animal moving in real time (warm life).

---

## SECTION 3 — WHERE THEY LIVE (1:50 – 3:00) · Set-Up · Promise of the Premise

**What we see.** Wide drone-style plates (sourced or AI-generated from real Touran reference imagery) of the four protected areas: **Touran**, **Miandasht**, **Naybandan**, **Kavir**. Each named in a lower-third in Persian and English. A single ground-level still of a dry riverbed at dawn. Camera-trap footage of a male Yuz drinking, three a.m., infrared green. A silhouetted DOE game-guard inside a Land Cruiser, profile against windshield glare — *no spoken interview*. The narrator delivers his biography from above: "Twenty-one years in this reserve. Three confirmed sightings."

**What we hear.** Narrator describes elevation (1,000–3,000 m), temperature swings (snow to 45°C), prey species (jebeer gazelle, urial, hare). **First entry of score** — a sparse, sustained, single piano note motif that will return at Pirouz's death.

**Emotional beat.** Wonder, but cool — the audience falls in love with a landscape before they are asked to grieve it.

**Pacing.** Slowest section so far. Long holds on landscape stills, 6–8 seconds each.

**Section grammar:** opens on aerial wide (the world), closes on a single male at a water source (the inhabitant).

---

## SECTION 4 — WHAT WENT WRONG (3:00 – 5:00) · Catalyst → Bad Guys Close In

**What we see.** Historical archive in sequence: black-and-white Qajar / Sassanid hunting imagery (royal cheetah-hunt miniatures, the *yuz* as trained companion); colour photograph of the last Indian cheetahs, 1947; news footage from the 1979 revolution (street scenes, not violence); Iran-Iraq war footage (long shots, dust, no faces); contemporary aerial of the Tehran–Mashhad highway slicing through arid terrain at sunset; one held still of a Yuz carcass at a roadside, framed wide, four seconds, no longer.

**What we hear.** Narrator delivers the historical chain plainly. No moralising. *"The killing began as a sport. Then it became a habit. Then it became a matter of roads."* The score drops out entirely for the roadside still — only wind and the distant sound of a passing truck.

**Emotional beat.** Horror, but controlled. The audience is given enough to understand, never enough to look away from.

**Pacing.** Quickening through history, then a hard slow-down for the roadside image. The held silence is the punctuation.

**Section grammar:** opens on historical hunt artwork (the past), closes on a recovery image — a live Yuz in daylight (the present's stubborn fact).

---

## SECTION 5 — THE NAMED CHEETAHS (5:00 – 7:00) · B Story / Midpoint All-Is-Lost

**What we see.** Six portrait cards, one per cheetah, each held 8–10 seconds with a single photograph and a name card in Persian and English:

- **Kooshki** (کوشکی) — male, captured young (2007), founder of the captive programme.
- **Delbar** (دلبر) — female, "the one who took her time."
- **Iran** (ایران) — female, named for her country; first to give birth in captivity.
- **Koushki II** (کوشکی دوم) — male of the next generation.
- **Telma** (تلما) — matriarch, the collared female whose data drew the map.

Then a slow transition into the **Pirouz** sequence: a still of the newborn in a gloved human hand, dated **1 May 2022**. The two-week handoff frame — gloved hand with syringe — held one full beat in silence for the two siblings who did not survive. Brief Iranian state-TV-style graphics archive (lower thirds, news tickers). Children's drawings collected from social media. A clip of a vet weighing him. Pirouz at four weeks, three months, six months (the iconic feather-toy frame the country fell in love with), nine months.

**At ~6:30 — the All-Is-Lost beat.** A single still photograph of an empty veterinary incubator, lit by a fluorescent tube. Held for nine full seconds. The piano motif returns; a **single sustained cello note** enters under it — Pirouz's signature, the film's first warmth. Narrator, in a register half a step quieter: *"He was named Victorious. He lived ten months."* Cut to a final still of Pirouz alive, looking past camera. Hold three seconds in silence.

**Emotional beat.** This is the film's emotional centre. Investment → loss. The audience earns the right to grieve here because they have just spent two minutes learning to love six specific animals.

**Pacing.** Slowest sequence in the film. The empty incubator is the longest held shot in the cut.

**Section grammar:** opens on portrait cards (the named living), closes on the empty incubator (the named absent).

---

## SECTION 6 — CONSERVATION TODAY (7:00 – 8:30) · Dark Night → Break into Three

**What we see.** Archive: a news photograph of Dr **Kavous Seyed-Emami** (the conservation biologist who died in custody in 2018) — public photographs only, treated with restraint. A still of camera-trap equipment in a Pardisan Park laboratory. A map graphic showing the protected areas with overlay icons for three pressures: sanctions (red icon, a packing crate stamped HELD at customs), conflict 2026 (orange), road kills (black). A white-on-black pull-quote card — four seconds — attributed to an ICS biologist: *"Every camera we put in a tree is an act of patience."* Camera-trap footage of an unidentified Yuz moving at dawn, then daylight drone of the empty wadi.

**What we hear.** Narrator names the three pressures — sanctions, the imprisonment and death of scientists, the 2026 regional war — without editorial. *"Their crime was a camera in a tree."* The score returns, slightly warmer, in low strings. Then a single line, lifted in register: *"And still — in a riverbed at dawn, in a place no one is watching — she is still here."*

**Emotional beat.** Compounded grief, but a turn toward the next breath. The audience is being prepared to be asked something.

**Pacing.** Brisker than Section 5 — the film is moving toward its question.

**Section grammar:** opens on a portrait of a dead human (Seyed-Emami), closes on a living cheetah in infrared (the survival of the watched).

---

## SECTION 7 — OUTRO (8:30 – 9:30) · Break into Three · Final Image · Mehrdad on Camera

**What we see.** Cut to **Mehrdad**, mid-shot, 50mm, soft daylight from a window left, looking directly into the lens. No lower-third. He speaks for about 30 seconds. Cut away from him to the film's final piece of footage: **real camera-trap video of a wild female with five cubs** moving in single file along a ridge at dusk, Touran, recent. The mother glances once back at her cubs. Cut to a final title card on black:

> **Kooshki · Delbar · Iran · Koushki · Telma · Pirouz (2022 — 2023)**
>
> ***___________***
>
> Fewer than 30 remain. Five were born this year.

**What we hear.** Mehrdad's own voice, not professionally graded — slightly closer mic, room tone audible. Score absent under his face. The cello note returns under the wild-cubs footage, joined by a single sustained warm chord that holds through the title card. Last sound: wind on the ridge. Three seconds of held black.

**Emotional beat.** Direct address → call → fragile hope. Not triumph. A question handed to the audience.

**Pacing.** Mehrdad's section is the only part of the film that breathes at conversational speed. The film ends on the longest held black of the cut.

**Section grammar:** opens on the only human face in the film (the responsibility), closes on a wild mother with five cubs and a blank seventh line (the unfinished sentence).

## Visual & Sonic Discipline (cross-cutting rules)

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| No on-camera interviews | Kapadia method. Narrator + archive carry the argument. The only spoken human face is Mehrdad in Section 7. |
| The word "we / us" appears once | At 8:30 in Mehrdad's address. Earned by total absence elsewhere. |
| Music enters three times only | Single piano in Sec 3; cello + piano under Pirouz in Sec 5; warm chord under final cubs in Sec 7. |
| Camera-trap footage is sacred | Used as-shot. Grain is credibility. No stabilisation, no over-grading, no crop past 20%. |
| Every section opens on landscape, closes on a face | The grammar of the film. Maintain through rough cut. |
| Latin name said once | At 1:00. Persian *Yuz* thereafter. |
| Negative prompt is global | No acacia, no savanna, no zebras, no lions. The visual rule is also the prompt rule. |
| The seventh blank line | The title card leaves space for a name we do not yet know. This is the film's argument in punctuation. |
