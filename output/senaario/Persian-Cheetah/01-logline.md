# Logline — The Last Cheetah You've Never Heard Of
## یوز — آخرین‌ها

**Slug:** `Persian-Cheetah`
**Runtime:** 9 minutes 30 seconds
**Format:** 16:9 documentary essay (Kapadia archival method + Varda-style essayist outro)
**Voice:** BBC omniscient narrator (calm, female, Attenborough cadence) → personal (Mehrdad) in outro
**Mode (Nichols):** Expository + Performative hybrid
**Method:** Archive + camera-trap + AI-generated landscape + still photography. **No interviews.**

---

## Primary Logline

> In a country most viewers have never associated with cheetahs, fewer than thirty Asiatic cheetahs remain alive — and the world's only refuge for this 30,000-year-old subspecies hangs on six names the world was never told: Kooshki, Delbar, Iran, Koushki, Telma, and a cub called Pirouz who lived ten months and broke a nation's heart.

## Alternate Loglines (defensive — for pitch decks of different lengths)

**One-liner:** Twelve breathing cats on a stone plateau, and a single name — Pirouz — that an entire country mourned.

**Twitter:** The fastest land animal you've never heard of is Iranian, critically endangered, and going extinct quietly. This is what *quietly* sounds like.

---

## Controlling Idea

> **A species does not disappear in silence; it disappears in anonymity. To name the last individuals is to refuse their erasure — and to make their survival the audience's responsibility.**

Every beat of the film either *names* (the six animals, the dead scientist, the title card) or *withholds a name* (the African cheetah of the opening; the unnamed wild mother of the closing). The argument is structural, not rhetorical.

---

## Genre & Lineage

- **Primary mode:** Conservation essay-documentary in the Attenborough / BBC *Planet Earth* tradition.
- **Structural debt:** Asif Kapadia's archival-only method (*Senna*, *Amy*, *Maradona*) — no on-camera interviews. The voices are the narration, the silences, and the archive itself.
- **Outro mode:** Varda / Herzog participatory — the filmmaker briefly steps into the frame, in his own room, in his own voice, and hands the question to the audience.
- **Reference touchstones:** Werner Herzog, *Encounters at the End of the World* (the essayist's first-person); Joshua Oppenheimer's restraint when handling national grief; the silence-discipline of Sohrab Shahid-Saless.

## Audience & Why Now

- **Primary audience:** international (English-speaking), conservation-curious, BBC/Netflix nature documentary viewer.
- **Secondary audience:** Iranian diaspora and Persian-speaking conservationists who already know the names and need the film to honour them correctly.
- **Why now:** the 2023 death of Pirouz, the dwindling census (≈12 confirmed in 2023), and the 2026 regional war that is again pulling budget and attention away from the reserves — together create a closing window. If this film does not exist in 2026, it may not have a subject in 2030.

## The Promise to the Audience

We will show them a cheetah they have never seen on screen, a landscape they have never associated with the word *cheetah*, and a single ten-month-old cub whose name they will not forget. We will not ask for money. We will ask for memory.
