# Beat Sheet — The Last Cheetah You've Never Heard Of
## یوز — آخرین‌ها

**Model:** Save-the-Cat adapted for 9:30 documentary essay. Page references replaced with timecode.

---

## 1 · Opening Image (0:00 – 0:08)
African cheetah at full sprint across golden savanna. Dust, gazelle, the postcard image the audience has been trained to expect. This is the film's *false* opening — the world the audience thinks they're in. Its function is to be overturned. The final image (cubs on an Iranian ridge) will mirror and answer it.

## 2 · Theme Stated (0:30 — 1:50)
Spoken twice, obliquely. First as the misdirection closes: *"You are imagining the wrong one."* Then in Section 2 as the comparative-biology sequence lands: *"This is a cat that the world forgot it had."* The film's argument: **a species disappears when it becomes anonymous; naming refuses erasure.**

## 3 · Setup — The World (1:50 – 3:00)
The world the Yuz lives in. Four protected areas named (Touran, Miandasht, Naybandan, Kavir). Elevation, climate, prey. Camera-trap footage of a single male establishes that the species is real, alive, *present*. The audience is taught the geography before they are asked to grieve its damage. **The piano motif is planted here** — to be paid off at Pirouz's death.

## 4 · Catalyst — The Wound (3:00 – 5:00)
What went wrong, in a chain: Qajar hunting → Indian extinction 1947 → 1979 revolution disruption → Iran-Iraq war → road infrastructure → vehicle collision as the dominant cause of death today. The roadside-carcass still is the inciting incident of the audience's emotional involvement. After this shot, the film is no longer descriptive — it is a story with stakes.

## 5 · B Story — The Names (5:00 – 6:00)
The five named cheetahs introduced as portraits: Kooshki, Delbar, Iran, Koushki, Telma. The B story carries the theme — *every one of these animals has a name, and naming is what saves them*. This section also seeds the audience with affection that will be cashed in at the midpoint.

## 6 · Fun & Games / Promise of the Premise (6:00 – 6:30)
Pirouz's birth, **1 May 2022**. The premise the film opened with — *we can keep these animals alive* — performed in full. Pirouz on Iranian state TV. Children's drawings. The vet's gloved hand. The audience is allowed to feel hope. This is the **only sustained warmth in the film**. Beneath it, one silent beat for the two cubs who died at fourteen days — the **payoff price** that prepares Pirouz's loss.

## 7 · Midpoint / All-Is-Lost (≈ 6:30)
**Pirouz dies of kidney failure at ten months.** Empty incubator, held nine seconds. The piano motif returns. The cello signature enters — Pirouz's, and only his. Narrator: *"He was named Victorious. He lived ten months."* Structurally, this is *both* midpoint reversal *and* all-is-lost — compressed because the runtime is 9:30, not 110 minutes. The audience has been led specifically here for this loss.

## 8 · Bad Guys Close In → Dark Night of the Soul (7:00 – 8:30)
Sanctions starve the veterinary supply chain. Kavous Seyed-Emami's death in custody, 2018. Other scientists imprisoned. The 2026 regional war diverts budget and attention. The conservation programme has lost: a cub, its scientists, its funding, and the world's attention. This is the film's lowest point. The audience is held in it for a full ninety seconds — long enough to feel it, short enough not to lose them. Then a single hinge line: *"And still — in a riverbed at dawn, in a place no one is watching — she is still here."*

## 9 · Break into Three (8:30)
The narrator falls silent. **Mehrdad** appears on camera — the only human face in the film — and delivers the film's call: *if you do not know her name, she will go quietly.* This is the only first-person plural ("us / we") moment in the script. Earned by withholding.

## 10 · Finale (8:30 – 9:20)
Cut to real camera-trap footage of a **wild female with five cubs** on a Touran ridge. The mother glances back once. The cubs move in single file at dusk. This is the film's argument-against-despair: the named are mostly dead; the unnamed are mostly alive.

## 11 · Final Image (9:20 – 9:30)
Title card lists the six names with Pirouz's dates, then a **blank seventh line**. Beneath: *Fewer than 30 remain. Five were born this year.* The opening image of an African cheetah on golden grass is answered by an Iranian mother and five cubs on grey stone. The seventh blank line is the audience's responsibility, written in punctuation.

## Controlling Idea (proof)

> *A species does not disappear in silence; it disappears in anonymity.*

Each section's relationship to the idea:
1. **Cold open** — *the consequence of anonymity* (fewer than thirty).
2. **What it is** — *the name reclaimed* (Yuz).
3. **Landscape** — *where the choices happen* (highways, mines, livestock).
4. **What went wrong** — *the choices already made* (Qajar hunt → highway).
5. **The names** — *the cost of anonymity defied* (six names; one beloved cub).
6. **Conservation today** — *the choices not yet made* (the wild cubs, the patient cameras).
7. **The choice** — *the address to the audience* (the blank seventh line).

The idea threads through every section. Verdict: **the film knows what it is about.**

---

## Why this works as a 9:30 documentary

A feature documentary on the Asiatic cheetah would dilute the names across an hour. A 9:30 essay forces every shot to do load-bearing work — and forces the audience to leave the film able to *recite* six names. That recitation is the conservation act. The brevity is not a constraint; it is the strategy.
