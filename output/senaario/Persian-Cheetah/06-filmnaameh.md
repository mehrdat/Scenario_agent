## SECTION 2 — WHAT MAKES THEM DIFFERENT (1:00 – 1:50)

**[SCENE 08 · 1:00–1:18 · GRAPHIC + PHOTO split: African (left) and Asiatic (right) side by side]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
She is leaner than her African cousin.
Longer-legged. Paler. Her winter coat is thicker —
because her winters are colder.

Her Latin name is *Acinonyx jubatus venaticus*.
We will not use it again.

**[SCENE 09 · 1:18–1:30 · GRAPHIC: range map — historical range from the Arabian Peninsula to India]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
A century ago, this cat ran from the Arabian Peninsula —
to India.

**[SCENE 10 · 1:30–1:42 · GRAPHIC: range map retracts; India 1947 caption fades; range collapses to central Iran]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
In 1947, India lost hers.

Today, every one of them is here.

## SECTION 4 — WHAT WENT WRONG (3:00 – 5:00)

**[SCENE 17 · 3:00–3:18 · PHOTO: Sassanid/Safavid hunting miniature — yuz on horseback as trained royal cheetah]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
For two thousand years, kings rode beside her.
They called her **یوز** —
and they trained her to hunt with them.

**[SCENE 17a · 3:18–3:30 · PHOTO: Qajar-era hunting photograph, black-and-white]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
The killing began as a sport.
Then it became a habit.
Then it became a matter of roads.

**[SCENE 18 · 3:30–3:42 · PHOTO: 1947 — the last Indian cheetahs, archival]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
In 1947, India lost hers.
Iran was the last country on Earth that still had this animal alive in the wild.

**[SCENE 19 · 3:42–4:00 · ARCHIVE: 1979 revolution street scenes — no violence, no faces]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
In 1979, the country turned over.
For a year, no one watched the reserves.

**[SCENE 20 · 4:00–4:18 · ARCHIVE: Iran–Iraq war, long lens, dust, no faces]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
For eight years after that, no one had the budget.

**[SCENE 21 · 4:18–4:36 · AI VIDEO: aerial of Tehran–Mashhad highway at sunset, two lanes cutting across plateau]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
And then —
we built the road.

The deadliest predator of the Asiatic cheetah today
is a truck at night.

## SECTION 5 — THE NAMED CHEETAHS (5:00 – 7:00)

**[SCENE 24 · 5:00–5:12 · PHOTO + NAME CARD: Kooshki / کوشکی]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
**Kooshki.**
A male.
Captured young, in 2007 — when herders shot his mother.
The founder of a programme that began with the question:
*Can we keep them alive ourselves?*

He died in 2018.
Kidney failure.

**[SCENE 25 · 5:12–5:24 · PHOTO + NAME CARD: Delbar / دلبر]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
**Delbar.**
The one who took her time.
For years, she would not breed.
And then, against every expectation, she did.

**[SCENE 26 · 5:24–5:36 · PHOTO + NAME CARD: Iran / ایران]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
**Iran.**
They named her for her country.
She would be the first.

**[SCENE 27a · 5:36–5:48 · PHOTO + NAME CARD: Koushki II / کوشکی دوم]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
**Koushki the Second.**
A male of the next generation.

**[SCENE 27b · 5:48–6:00 · PHOTO + NAME CARD: Telma / تلما]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
**Telma.**
The matriarch.

A collar around her neck, for two years,
drew the first true map of how a Yuz uses her land.

We learned more from Telma
than from a century of hunters.

**[SCENE 28 · 6:00–6:12 · PHOTO + ARCHIVE: Pirouz, 1 May 2022, gloved hand cradling newborn]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
And on the first of May, in 2022 —

**Pirouz.**
*Victorious.*

The first Asiatic cheetah ever born in captivity.

**[SCENE 28a · 6:12–6:18 · PHOTO: gloved hand with syringe — no cub visible]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
*(quieter)*
Two of the three did not see fourteen days.

*(one second of silence held on the gloved hand. The syringe is the cubs.)*

**[SCENE 29 · 6:18–6:30 · PHOTO MONTAGE: Pirouz at 4 wk → 3 mo → 6 mo (feather toy) → 9 mo, Ken Burns push on each]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
A country that had not had good news about its wildlife in a generation
followed his weight gain on the evening news.

Children drew him.
For three hundred days,
he was a small, public miracle.

**[SCENE 30 · 6:30–6:42 · PHOTO: empty veterinary incubator under fluorescent light, held twelve seconds]**

*(piano motif returns, single notes. A **single sustained cello note** enters under — Pirouz's signature. The film's first warmth.)*

**[SCENE 30a · 6:42–6:48]**

NARRATOR (V.O.)
*(half a step quieter)*
He was named Victorious.
He lived ten months.

**[SCENE 31 · 6:48–7:00 · PHOTO: Pirouz alive, looking past camera]**
