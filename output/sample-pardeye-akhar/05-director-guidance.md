# Director's Bible — پرده‌ی آخر / The Final Curtain

---

## 1. Visual grammar

### Shot-size policy per emotional beat

| Emotional beat | Default shot size | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Maryam in her world (SC 1, 2, 7) | MS / MLS | The room and the alley are characters; she shares the frame with them. |
| Maryam in transit (SC 2, 6) | LS / MLS, tracking or static | Her smallness in the city is the point. |
| Maryam asking, listening (SC 3) | MS, locked | She is in conversation with an off-screen voice; her face does the work. |
| Maryam discovering (SC 4) | MS to MCU progression across the scene | The first true close-up of the film. |
| Maryam reading (SC 5) | CU + ECU on hands and letter | The emotional peak; we earn the close. |
| Maryam at the river (SC 6) | LS, then MS | She is small against the dry channel; we step closer only when she speaks. |
| Maryam closing the box (SC 7) | ECU on hands, then MS on her seated figure | The hands are the protagonist of this scene. |
| Final image (SC 8) | LS on the room, no figure | The absence is the subject. |

### Lens choices

- **Hero lens**: 50mm prime (Cooke S4 or Zeiss Super Speed). Used for SC 1, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8.
- **Wide**: 35mm for SC 2 (the alley) and SC 6 (the riverbed). Never wider.
- **Long**: 85mm for the two close-ups of the letter in SC 4–5. Compression isolates the letter from the courtyard.
- **No zoom**. Ever.
- **Open aperture for interiors** (T2 or T2.2) — shallow depth, soft falloff. **Closed aperture for exteriors** (T5.6 or T8) — depth, geographic clarity.

### Camera movement philosophy

The camera moves **three times in the film**. No more.

1. **SC 2** — a slow lateral tracking shot beside Maryam as she walks the alley. Speed: her walking pace. Distance: 6 meters from her shoulder.
2. **SC 4** — a slow dolly-in (or precision-slider) on the letter as Maryam unfolds it. Eight seconds, from 90 cm to 45 cm.
3. **SC 6** — a slow push-in on Maryam standing at the bridge parapet, from MLS to MS. Six seconds.

Every other shot is locked. The discipline is the point. When the camera moves in this film, the audience knows something has just changed.

### Composition rules

- **Thirds, not center.** Maryam usually sits on a vertical third (left in interiors, right in exteriors — see *screen direction* below). Center framing is reserved for SC 8 (the empty room).
- **Frame within frame** in SC 3 (the beaded curtain), SC 4 (the arched portal in the background), SC 8 (the window inside the wide).
- **Depth layers**: foreground / midground / background populated in every exterior. The bazaar has a stall (FG), a passerby (MG), an arch (BG). The courtyard has a cypress (FG), the bench (MG), the dome (BG).
- **Negative space**: SC 6 and SC 8 use empty negative space. SC 3 and SC 7 are filled.

## 2. Performance direction — directing the actress

### What to ask for

- **Stillness.** Practice with her: she sits on a chair for two minutes without moving. We learn together what her resting face looks like. That face is the film's most-used image.
- **Specificity of action.** When she pours the tea, she has done this thousands of times. When she opens the letter, she has never done this. The difference must be visible in the hands.
- **Listening over speaking.** In SC 3, she listens to a voice she cannot see. Direct her to imagine the speaker's age, body, the room behind the curtain. The listening is the performance.
- **One private gesture per scene.** Identify one small, private thing she does each scene that no one but the audience will notice: the way she adjusts the chador under her chin in SC 1; the way she touches the blue paint in SC 3 (if we keep that beat); the way she places her palm on the closed book in SC 4 before opening it. These are the signature of a real person.

### What NOT to ask for

- **Do not ask for tears.** If they come, fine; if they don't, fine. Never reach for them.
- **Do not ask for "weight."** Asking an actress to play "heaviness" produces performance. Ask instead for specificity of action.
- **Do not over-rehearse the V.O. listening.** SC 5 should be the first time she hears the V.O. played back. The first listen is the most honest.
- **Do not give her the letter's content to memorize.** Let her read it on camera, for the first time, in the take. The first read is the take.

### Rehearsal notes

- **Two weeks before**: full read-through of the script with the actress. No staging. She reads the V.O. once, then never again until the day.
- **One week before**: walk the locations with her. No camera. Let her find her own routes through each space.
- **Day before**: blocking only. Mark, walk, mark, walk. No emotional work.
- **Shoot day**: minimal direction. One sentence per take maximum. "Slower." "More private." "Don't push." Never give a result direction ("be sadder").

## 3. Scene-by-scene direction

### SC 1 — The room at dawn
Begin with the curtain — three seconds of curtain moving in pre-dawn light before we cut to Maryam in bed. Hold her in bed for ten seconds. She does not sit up immediately. When she rises, the camera does not move. Three setups total: bed, basin, chair. Cut on action only. The "بلند شو مادر" line: have her *not* say it on take one. See if the silence is stronger. Decide in the edit, not on the day.

### SC 2 — The alley walk
One master tracking shot, plus three inserts (the pamphlet, the kite, the samovar shop). Walk her the full length of the alley once, no marks. Watch her natural pace. Match the tracking to that pace exactly. If she pauses, the camera pauses. The pamphlet insert is two seconds — no more. The audience should register it without reading it.

### SC 3 — The bookseller
Lock the camera at MS on Maryam, beaded curtain visible over her left shoulder. The off-screen actor (the son) performs from behind the curtain, in real time, on every take. Do not loop in post. The faint hesitation between his lines is the texture. After his second line ("Mrs. Forouzesh… green door"), hold on Maryam for five seconds. Let her absorb. Then she turns. Do not cut on her line of departure — let her walk out of frame. Hold the empty MS for three seconds. Beaded curtain still.

### SC 4 — The courtyard, the find
Begin wide — Maryam entering frame at far left, the bench at the far right, the dome above. Hold the wide for the duration of her crossing (roughly 20 seconds in 16-meter courtyard). Cut to MS only when she sits. The dolly-in on the letter (the second moving shot of the film) begins after she has read the first line. Eight seconds. End on ECU of the letter in her hands. Do not cut to her face yet — hold on the letter, then cut to her face only after the V.O. has begun in SC 5.

### SC 5 — The letter and the memory
The most fragile scene. Cover three angles only: (1) MS Maryam reading, (2) CU her face, (3) ECU letter. The flash-images are pre-shot, pre-graded inserts — *do not* shoot them on the same day. Different stock, different light, different texture. Cut to a flash-image *only* after a V.O. line completes. The image should feel like an answer to the line, not an illustration. Hold each flash-image for 2–3 seconds. No music. The first muezzin call begins in the last 15 seconds of the scene, very distant.

### SC 6 — The river
Begin with a static LS — Maryam in lower-right third, the dry channel stretching to upper-left, Khaju Bridge in deep background. Hold 12 seconds. She walks into the frame, stops at the parapet. The push-in (third and final camera move of the film) begins as she places her hand on the stone. Six seconds, ending in MS. She delivers "تو هم رفتی" — a whisper, almost to herself, not to the camera. Hold three seconds after the line. Cut.

### SC 7 — The box
This scene is about hands. Three setups: (1) Maryam seated on the floor, MS; (2) ECU on the open box and her hands; (3) MS as she rises, crosses to window, sits. The brass-clasp click is the sonic peak of the film. Foley it. After the click, hold the closed box in frame for four full seconds before she rises. She does not look at the chair. She does not speak to the chair. The chair stays empty.

### SC 8 — The final image
One setup. The same angle as SC 1's wide. The curtain moves. The chair is empty. The other chair is empty. The book of Hafez on Maryam's chair, closed. Hold for ten seconds before any light change. Then dial the light from pre-dawn blue to dawn amber over thirty seconds — practical lighting on a dimmer or in-camera through ND shift. When the light has fully arrived, hold three more seconds in silence. Then fade. The title cards arrive after a full second of black.

## 4. Continuity rules

### Light direction
**Upper-left, throughout the film.** The morning sun, the courtyard's afternoon angle, the river's late light, the window in SC 8 — all are upper-left in frame. This is non-negotiable. The only exception: the flash-images in SC 5, which are deliberately disoriented (upper-right or flat).

### Screen direction
- Maryam moves **left-to-right** when going toward the letter (SC 1 → SC 4).
- She moves **right-to-left** when returning home (SC 6 → SC 7).
- In SC 6, she stops at the bridge facing right (toward the future, toward the river that is leaving).
- In SC 8, the window is on the right side of the frame. The light comes from the upper-left. She is not present, but the geography remembers her.

### Chador continuity
- The chador is the **same single chador** in every scene. Black, slightly worn at the hem, no decoration.
- She wears it from SC 1 (donned at the door) through SC 7 (removed inside the room, off-camera).
- In SC 8, the chador is on its hook on the door — visible at the edge of the wide frame. The chador's presence in the empty room is the final character beat.
- The chador never slides off her head. She adjusts it once in SC 1, once in SC 4. Both adjustments are private gestures.

### Props continuity
- **The wooden box** in SC 7 must match exactly any glimpse of it in earlier scenes (none currently — but if we add a cold open, the box appears there too).
- **The book of Hafez** in SC 4 is the same physical book in SC 6 (carried under the chador), SC 7 (placed beside the box), and SC 8 (on the empty chair).
- **The letter** is a single yellowed sheet, one fold. The seal is broken in SC 4. The same letter is in the box in SC 7.

### Sound continuity
- **The muezzin** is the same voice across all three calls. Recorded once, used three times.
- **The wind** in exteriors is field-recorded from the actual locations. No library sound.

## 5. Post-production notes

### Editing rhythm
- **Long takes preserved.** The default cut is the master. Cut to coverage only when the master fails or a beat demands it.
- **Average shot length**: target 14 seconds (long for a 17-minute short).
- **No L-cuts or J-cuts in SC 4–5.** These scenes cut clean. Audio bleeds break the contemplative frame.
- **L-cut allowed** at the SC 5 → SC 6 transition: the V.O. of the letter's last line bleeds two seconds into the wide of the river.
- **Hard cut to black** after SC 8's fade — no cross-dissolve to the title card.

### Sound mix priorities
1. **Voice clarity** of the V.O. letter. The father's voice must sit forward in the mix, intimate, low-passed slightly to suggest age and recording on old paper.
2. **The muezzin** — present but never dominant. Mix at -18dB peak.
3. **The brass-clasp click** in SC 7 — see above. Designed event.
4. **Wind and ambient room tone** — continuous bed under every scene, never absent except in the three designed silences.
5. **Score** — see FILE 6. Subordinate to voice and ambient.

### Color grading
- One grading session, on a calibrated reference monitor, with DP and director both present.
- Three palette LUTs prepared (dawn-interior, day-city, golden-courtyard). Apply, then hand-tune scene by scene.
- The flash-images in SC 5 are graded *last*, deliberately to look like a different film stock — slightly bleached, higher contrast, lifted blacks.
- Skin tone: hold to the warmer side of neutral, never push amber.

### Reference stills to seek before grading
- *Where Is the Friend's House?* (1987) — the school courtyard sequences.
- *Through the Olive Trees* (1994) — the hillside-to-village shots for amber/green relationship.
- *Taste of Cherry* (1997) — late-afternoon dust palette.
- *A Separation* (2011) — interior light through curtains.
- Sebastião Salgado, *Workers* — for the relationship between dust and skin.
- Vermeer interior light studies — for SC 8's amber-blue transition.
