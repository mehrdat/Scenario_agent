# Director's Recommendations & Production Notes
## "پرده‌ی آخر / The Final Curtain"

---

## Top 10 production decisions to get right

1. **Cast Maryam first, then design everything around her face.** The film is sixty percent close-ups of an aging Iranian woman who never weeps. Without the right actress, no production design saves it. Allocate the largest casting budget here.

2. **Shoot in real Isfahan, not a constructed set.** The dust, the dome's specific turquoise, the bazaar's vaulted brick — these are not reproducible. Budget five days of location shooting in Isfahan, plus two for interiors that can be a controlled location anywhere.

3. **Late-afternoon/golden-hour for SC 4–6.** Schedule the courtyard, the river, and Maryam's window for the 90-minute window before sunset. This means SC 4 across two days (light moves fast).

4. **One lens family throughout.** Commit to a vintage Cooke or Zeiss Super Speeds prime set (35mm, 50mm, 85mm). The unified glass character holds the film together more than any color grade can.

5. **No score under V.O.** The letter scene (SC 5) must play with ambient sound only — pigeons, distant azan, the wind. Score enters only after the letter is folded.

6. **One muezzin, recorded fresh.** Do not use library azan. Commission a single elderly muezzin to record three calls (dawn, afternoon, dusk) on location. The continuity of voice across the film is a structural choice.

7. **The brass-clasp click in SC 7 is a designed event.** Foley this in post, mix at -8dB peak. It is the loudest sound in the film. The audience will not consciously notice; they will feel the ending land.

8. **Title in Farsi first, then English.** پرده‌ی آخر appears alone for two seconds before "The Final Curtain" fades in beneath. Order matters.

9. **No music for the trailer's first 30 seconds.** Sell the silence. Foreign audiences expect silence from Iranian cinema; deliver it.

10. **Color grade once on a calibrated reference monitor with the director of photography in the room.** Do not grade remotely. The amber-to-blue transitions across SC 4 → SC 8 are the film's chromatic argument.

## Casting notes for Maryam

**The role demands**:
- An actress between 60 and 72.
- A face that can hold a static close-up for 30 seconds without performing.
- Native Tehrani or Isfahani Farsi; comfortable with literary register for the brief V.O. lines.
- Physical bearing: upright but with a slight, *earned* stoop (not performed — habitual).
- A history of stage work is a plus; theater actresses know how to sit still.

**Shortlist** (Iranian actresses appropriate to the role — verify availability):
- **Fatemeh Motamed-Arya** (b. 1340) — has worked with Beizai, Mehrjui. Possesses the dignity and the restraint.
- **Susan Taslimi** (b. 1329, diaspora) — if international co-production is feasible; a different register but unmatched gravitas.
- **Rakhshan Banietemad** as actress (rare) or as casting consultant — she knows every face in this age range.
- **Pari Saberi** for theatrical bearing.
- **Mahaya Petrosian** at the younger end of the range.

**Audition exercise**: hand the candidate the letter from SC 5. Ask her to read it silently for two minutes on camera. Do not give direction. The actress who can survive that take is the actress.

**Do not cast**: a younger actress aged up with prosthetic. The neck and the hands always betray. The film cannot afford this lie.

## Location scouting checklist — Isfahan

**SC 1, 7, 8 — Maryam's room.** Required: a small room (15–20 m²) with one window facing east. Whitewashed adobe or plaster walls. Wooden ceiling beams visible. A working oil lamp (or convincing prop). A samovar. Two simple wooden chairs. Find in: old houses in Jolfa or the Khaju quarter. Three candidates needed; one principal, two backups.

**SC 2 — Old quarter alley.** Required: a narrow alley (2–3 m wide), adobe walls, one or two wooden doors with original hardware. Quiet (we can't dub everything). The walls must be paintable for the pamphlet dressing. Find in: Joubareh quarter or the southern fringes of the old city. Avoid alleys with visible satellite dishes.

**SC 3 — Bazaar.** Required: a small bookseller's stall in a vaulted section of the Bazaar-e Bozorg or Bazaar-e Honar. Beaded curtain at the back. Vendor consent for two days of shooting. Find via: the Isfahan Cultural Heritage Office; the city's bookseller guild.

**SC 4 — Safavid courtyard.** PRIORITY. Required: a Safavid-era four-iwan courtyard with intact tilework, one or two cypress trees, a stone bench (or place for one), a turquoise dome visible above the wall. Candidates: Madreseh-ye Chahar Bagh, Madreseh-ye Nim-Avard, Masjed-e Hakim courtyard, a private courtyard in Joubareh. Permits 6–8 weeks in advance.

**SC 6 — Zayandeh-Rud.** Required: a stretch of dry or low-water riverbed with Khaju Bridge visible. Time of year: late summer / early autumn when the river is low. Avoid: tourist sections at sunset (foot traffic).

**Backup days**: build two contingency days into the schedule for weather, light, and permit delays. Isfahan's autumn light is reliable but the wind is not.

## Shot philosophy

**Default**: the camera does not move. Static frames, locked on tripod or sturdy fluid head. The actress moves; the camera observes.

**When to move**:
- **SC 2** — one tracking shot beside Maryam at a respectful distance (the city is moving past her; the camera moves with her).
- **SC 4** — one slow dolly-in (or slider) on the letter as she unfolds it, 8 seconds, motivated by the audience leaning in.
- **SC 5** — no camera movement. Cuts only. The flash-images are still or near-still.
- **SC 8** — no movement. Hold.

**Earn the close-up**. The first close-up of Maryam's face arrives only in SC 4 (the bench). Before then: medium and long shots. The CU has emotional weight because we have waited for it.

**Single coverage where possible**. Many scenes are one shot. SC 1 may be three shots (bed, basin, chair). SC 6 may be one. Resist the impulse to over-cover.

**Eye-line**. Maryam's gaze is never directly into camera until the final beat of SC 8 — if at all. Reserve the lens-look for the moment of release.

## Sound design philosophy

**Layers**:
1. **Wind** — present in every exterior. Field-recorded in Isfahan, not generic.
2. **Muezzin** — three calls, single voice, three times in the film (SC 1 dawn, SC 5 afternoon end, SC 6/8 dusk).
3. **City murmur** — the bazaar in SC 3, the alley in SC 2. Field-recorded.
4. **Domestic** — samovar bubbling, paper unfolding, oil lamp wick, brass clasp.

**Score**: minimal. See FILE 6. No score over the V.O. letter.

**Silence**: SC 4 (the moment she lifts the stone), SC 7 (after the clasp clicks), the final three seconds of SC 8 before fade. These are absolute silences — even ambient stripped.

**Persian instruments**: ney (breath), tar (memory), nothing percussive. See FILE 6.

## Color grading approach

**Three palettes across the film**:

1. **Dawn / interior** (SC 1, 7, 8) — cool blue-white shifting to warm amber. Low contrast. The skin warm against cool walls.
2. **Day / city** (SC 2, 3) — neutral, slightly desaturated. The 1357 air is not nostalgic; it is real.
3. **Afternoon / courtyard / river** (SC 4, 5, 6) — golden hour, saturated amber, deep cypress greens, turquoise dome accent. This is the chromatic peak. The flash-images in SC 5 are over-exposed, grainy, slightly bleached.

**Skin tone**: never aggressively warmed. Iranian olive skin is its own correct color.

**Reference stills to seek**: *Where Is the Friend's House?* (Kiarostami), *Through the Olive Trees* (Kiarostami), the courtyard sequence in *The Color of Pomegranates* (Parajanov — color reference, not lighting), Sebastião Salgado's black-and-white tonal range applied to color.

## What to avoid (common mistakes in Iranian slow-cinema imitations)

- **Over-poeticizing the dialogue.** The Kiarostami test: would a real Isfahani woman say this in 1978? If the line is "too beautiful," it is wrong.
- **The wide drone shot of Isfahan at dawn.** Tempting, useless. The film is at street level.
- **Performed sadness.** Maryam never weeps. The actress and the director must agree on this in casting.
- **Symbol overload.** One symbol per scene. The doll (if added in SC 2), the bangle (referenced only), the dry river (one line), the empty chair (the through-line). Do not pile up.
- **Slow motion.** Never. The flash-images in SC 5 are full speed, just over-exposed and grainy.
- **Generic azan.** See above.
- **Reflexive Hafez quotation.** The book of Hafez is the witness, not the speaker. No verse is read aloud. The audience supplies the resonance.
- **Closing on the dome.** Tempting, generic. The film closes on the empty room.

## YouTube optimization notes

**Thumbnail moment**: SC 4 — Maryam's hand on the closed book of Hafez, turquoise dome out of focus behind, golden light. Vertical-crop friendly. Recognizable in 100×100 px.

**Pacing for online audience**: the 12-minute online cut should:
- Open with the cold-open (hand on the wooden box, 3 seconds, title).
- Trim SC 2 from 90 seconds to 45.
- Trim SC 7 by holding fewer beats.
- Keep SC 4–5 intact. The center must breathe.

**Chapter markers** (for YouTube description):
- 0:00 Opening
- 0:45 The Dawn
- 2:30 The Walk
- 4:00 The Bookseller
- 5:30 The Courtyard
- 7:00 The Letter
- 10:30 The River
- 12:30 The Box
- 14:30 The Window

**Title and description hooks**:
- Title: "The Final Curtain | پرده‌ی آخر — A short film from Isfahan, 1978"
- First line of description: a single sentence from the synopsis, in English and Farsi.
- Tags: Iranian cinema, slow cinema, Isfahan, Kiarostami tradition, short film, Persian language, 1978.

**Closed captions**: commission both English and Farsi captions, with literary translations rather than literal. Caption the V.O. letter carefully — the rhythm of the captions affects emotional pacing.

**Release strategy**: festival circuit first (six months minimum), then YouTube premiere with a director's introduction (60 seconds, Farsi with English subtitles).
