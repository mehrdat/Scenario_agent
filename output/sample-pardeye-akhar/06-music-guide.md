# Music & Sound Design Guide — پرده‌ی آخر

---

## Genre

**Persian classical + minimalist contemporary, with restraint.** This is not a film for the full *radif* in its traditional ornamented form. It is a film for the *bones* of Persian classical music — the breath of the ney, the patient pluck of the tar — placed inside the space of European minimalism (Pärt, Mompou, late Górecki). The hybrid creates emotional depth without ethnographic exoticism.

**Not appropriate**: Iranian pop, fusion with Western percussion, anything sweet, any setar trill in the Vahdat-vocal register. The film does not weep.

---

## Instrument palette

| Instrument | Emotional function | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Ney (Persian reed flute)** | Breath, longing, the soul — the most Persian sound for inwardness | SC 5 (after the letter folds), SC 8 (closing) |
| **Tar (long-necked lute)** | Memory, ancestral presence, the past speaking | SC 4 (entering courtyard), the flash-images of SC 5 |
| **Setar (smaller cousin of tar)** | Intimate, fragile, almost a whisper | SC 7 (placing letter in box) |
| **Kamancheh (spike fiddle)** | Lament without melodrama | Optional — only if Pärt-influenced bowed lines are required in SC 6 |
| **Cello (single, low register)** | Western anchor; grounds the Persian instruments | SC 5 underlay (very faint), SC 8 closing |
| **Tombak / daf** | **Absent.** No percussion in this film. |
| **Piano** | **Absent.** Wrong cultural register for 1357 Isfahan. |

## Intro music (opening 60 seconds)

**Mood**: anticipation, the silence before a long-awaited day.
**Tempo**: rubato, no fixed pulse. ~50 BPM if we had to name one.
**Instrument**: solo ney, breath audible, one long melodic phrase from the *Dastgah-e Shur* (دستگاه شور) — the modal home of melancholy and dawn. The phrase resolves not at the title, but four seconds *after* the title fades.
**Length**: 50 seconds, fading under the first ambient (curtain in pre-dawn wind).
**Reference track**: **Hassan Kassai — "Tasnif-e Shur"** (any solo ney recording from his catalog). The Kassai breath quality is the standard.
**Alternate**: a commissioned piece by **Hossein Omoumi** or **Hossein Alizadeh** (ney sections only).

## Background music — three scene-assigned cues

### Cue 1 — "Walking" (SC 2)
**Mood**: a city remembered.
**Instrument**: solo tar, low register, sparse single notes — *not* a melody, a pulse.
**Tempo**: matches Maryam's footstep cadence (roughly 60 BPM).
**Reference**: **Mohammad-Reza Lotfi — "Pas az Sokoot"** ("After Silence"). The opening minutes capture the right sparse pulse.
**Function**: present but almost subliminal. Fade out before SC 3.

### Cue 2 — "The Address" (SC 3, after Maryam leaves the bookseller)
**Mood**: the weight of news received.
**Instrument**: solo tar, slightly higher register, two notes repeated. Negative space dominates.
**Reference**: **Dariush Talai — "Radif of Mirza Abdollah"**, the slowest gushe-ha. Specifically the breathing pauses between phrases.
**Function**: 15 seconds only. Bridges to SC 4.

### Cue 3 — "Courtyard Entry" (SC 4, as Maryam crosses)
**Mood**: arrival at a sacred place.
**Instrument**: tar (memory) doubled an octave below by cello (anchor). Two notes per ten seconds.
**Reference**: **Arvo Pärt — "Spiegel im Spiegel"** (texture reference; do not use directly). For Persian-instrument equivalent: **Hossein Alizadeh — "Raz-o-Niyaz"**, slowed.
**Function**: enters as Maryam steps into the courtyard. Drops out completely when her hand touches the book. The silence of the find is the music.

## Turning point music — SC 4–5 (the letter)

**The most important musical decision in the film: silence under the V.O.**

While the father's voice reads the letter, there is **no music**. Only ambient — distant pigeons, the courtyard's air, far-off bazaar murmur.

Music re-enters in SC 5 *after* Maryam folds the letter and places it against her chest. A single ney phrase, six notes, breath audible. The phrase is the emotional release the audience needs after the silence under the V.O.

**Reference for the post-letter phrase**: **Hossein Omoumi — "Avaz-e Bayat-e Esfahan"** (the Isfahan modal system, named for this very city — the choice is structural, not decorative). Specifically the unaccompanied opening phrase of any of his Bayat-e Esfahan recordings.

---

## End music — final 2 minutes + credits

**SC 8 (final image, 90 seconds)**:
- 0–30s: silence (the held empty room).
- 30–60s: a slow cello note enters, single sustained tone, *do la* in the Bayat-e Esfahan mode.
- 60–90s: ney joins, *one* phrase, the same six notes from SC 5 — the film's musical signature. The phrase completes as the fade-to-black begins.

**Title card (پرده‌ی آخر / The Final Curtain)**:
- The ney's last breath sustains through the title, three seconds, then silence.

**Credits**:
- A new piece, finally with two voices — a tar and a ney in conversation, not in unison. This is the only moment of musical "company" in the film. About 90 seconds.
- **Reference for the credits piece**: **Hossein Alizadeh & Madjid Khaladj** collaborations (the tar conversations from *Endless Vision* or *Ney-Nava*, isolating the non-percussive sections).

## Reference composers / albums (5 specific)

1. **Hossein Alizadeh** — *Endless Vision* (2002, ECM-style production), and *Raz-o-Niyaz*. Tar tone reference. The contemporary Iranian classical master most aligned with this film's register.
2. **Mohammad-Reza Lotfi** — *Pas az Sokoot* ("After Silence"). The title alone explains the alignment. Sparse, contemplative, no excess.
3. **Hossein Omoumi** — solo ney recordings, especially in Dastgah-e Shur and Avaz-e Bayat-e Esfahan. Breath-forward, unornamented.
4. **Kayhan Kalhor** (kamancheh) — *Silent City* with Brooklyn Rider, for the East-West hybrid space if extended. Use only if budget allows commissioned work.
5. **Arvo Pärt** (Estonian, non-Iranian, included as a *texture* reference only) — *Tabula Rasa*, *Spiegel im Spiegel*. The tintinnabuli silence-architecture is the Western cousin of what Persian classical music's pauses do. Useful for the composer to study; not to use directly.

## Sound design (non-music) per scene

| Scene | Designed sounds | Field-recording priority |
|---|---|---|
| **SC 1** | Pre-dawn wind, distant muezzin (first call), samovar coming to boil, ceramic on wood, breath. | Field-record in an actual old Isfahan house at dawn. |
| **SC 2** | Footsteps on flagstone (dry), distant child's voice, paper-rustle of pamphlet in wind, far traffic, far protest murmur (very faint). | The protest murmur is the *only* political audio — recorded thin, mixed at -28dB. |
| **SC 3** | Bazaar ambient (vendors, footfall, distant copper hammering), the bookseller's son's breath behind the curtain, beaded curtain clack as Maryam departs. | The beaded-curtain clack is foleyed. The bazaar is field-recorded in Isfahan's Bazaar-e Bozorg. |
| **SC 4** | Wind through cypress (a specific sound — papery, dry), pigeons taking off, the smooth-stone lifted off paper (foleyed close), letter being unfolded (foleyed close). | The pigeon takeoff is critical — three flaps and the wind-disturbance after. |
| **SC 5** | The father's V.O., layered with the room-tone of an empty stone courtyard. Flash-images have *no* sound. The second muezzin call (the day's afternoon call) very distant at the end. | Father's V.O. recorded clean in a small wood-panelled room, no reverb added. |
| **SC 6** | Wind across an open riverbed (lower, broader than SC 4's wind), distant azan, stones underfoot, water-thread sound (almost subsonic). | Record actual Zayandeh-Rud ambient at dusk in low-water season. |
| **SC 7** | Wooden box opening, paper-on-paper, **brass-clasp click**, kettle whisper, oil-lamp wick. | The brass-clasp click is the foley centerpiece. Record a real Safavid-era box if possible. |
| **SC 8** | Curtain in wind, the dawn muezzin returning (third call — the cycle completes), absolute silence in the final three seconds. | The closing muezzin is the *same recording* as SC 1's. The cycle is exact. |

## Silence philosophy

**Three designed silences**:

1. **SC 4** — the four seconds after Maryam lifts the smooth stone off the letter. Ambient stripped to room-tone-only. The audience holds its breath with her.

2. **SC 7** — after the brass-clasp click closes the box. Four seconds of pure silence before the kettle resumes its whisper. The silence is the door closing on forty years.

3. **SC 8** — the final three seconds before fade to black. Even the wind drops. Even the ney has stopped. Pure silence. This silence is the film's argument: the answer to the letter was never going to be sound. It was going to be peace.

**Silence is louder than the muezzin in this film.** The mixer's job is to make the silences feel *constructed*, not accidental. Roll off the noise floor to -60dB in these three moments; do not insert *literal* digital zero (which sounds wrong in a theater). The silence should feel like a held breath, not a power failure.

---

## Practical notes for the composer

- One composer, one tar player, one ney player, one cellist. No ensemble.
- Recording: single condenser per instrument, no overdubs, no auto-tune, no compression beyond gentle bus.
- Total music run-time across the film: approximately **6 minutes** (out of 17). The rest is voice, ambient, and silence.
- The film's musical signature is the **six-note ney phrase** (composed fresh, in Bayat-e Esfahan). This phrase is heard three times: SC 5 (after the letter folds), SC 8 (at the fade), and the credits' final note. Three appearances, three altitudes — anticipation, release, memory.
