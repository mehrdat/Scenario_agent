# پرده‌ی آخر — The Final Curtain
**A short film by Daastansaraa Studio**
**Length:** 17 minutes
**Format:** 2.39:1 cinemascope, color
**Period:** Isfahan, Iran — Mehr 1357 (October 1978)

---

## Logline

**EN:** In Isfahan on the eve of revolution, a 65-year-old woman walks the city of her childhood looking for a letter from her exiled father — and when she finds it, she must decide whether the truth she has waited forty years for is one she can carry home.

**FA:** در اصفهانِ آستانه‌ی انقلاب، زنی شصت‌وپنج‌ساله شهرِ کودکی‌اش را به دنبالِ نامه‌ای از پدرِ تبعیدی‌اش می‌گردد — و وقتی نامه را می‌یابد، باید تصمیم بگیرد آیا حقیقتی که چهل سال در انتظارش بوده، حقیقتی است که می‌تواند به خانه بازگرداند.

---

## Synopsis

**Paragraph 1 — The world before.**
Maryam, sixty-five, has lived alone in a small house on the edge of the old quarter of Isfahan since her mother's death three years ago. Her father, a left-leaning newspaper editor, disappeared into exile in 1320 (1941) when she was a child of eight. For four decades she has kept a wooden box beneath her bed containing every letter that arrived — three in total, the last dated 1342 (1963). Yesterday, a neighbor's son returning from Paris brought word: a final letter exists. It was given to an old bookseller in the bazaar before her father died last winter. She did not know her father had died.

**Paragraph 2 — The walk.**
On a Thursday morning in autumn 1357, Maryam puts on her chador and walks out into a city she barely recognizes. The bazaar is full of pamphlets and rumors; soldiers stand at corners; the muezzin's call mixes with the murmur of protest. She moves through it as if through a dream — the city of her childhood overlaid on the city of the coming revolution. She finds the bookseller's son (off-screen, behind a beaded curtain); he tells her the letter was left with a neighbor, who has moved to a courtyard near the Chehel Sotoun. She walks there. She finds the letter folded inside a book of Hafez, on a stone bench under a cypress.

**Paragraph 3 — The reading and the return.**
Maryam reads. Her father's first line — *"پدر آمد و نیامد"* ("Father came and did not come") — is a riddle that will only open as the letter unfolds. He writes that he tried to return, in 1332 (1953), and stood at the end of her street, and turned back because he could not face her mother's grief. He writes that he loved her every day. He writes that the country he believed in does not exist, and never did. Maryam walks to the dry bed of the Zayandeh-Rud as the call to evening prayer begins. She does not weep. She returns home, places the letter in the wooden box with the others, and sits at the window as the light dies. The final image: the box closed, the window open, a curtain moving slowly in the wind.

## Screenplay

---

### SC 1 — INT. MARYAM'S ROOM — DAWN

*A small room. A single bed. A wooden chair by a low window. A samovar on a tray. The walls are bare except for a framed photograph turned face-down on a side table. The light is blue, pre-dawn.*

*MARYAM (65), sits up slowly in bed. She does not look at the clock. She knows the time from the light.*

*She rises. She crosses to the basin in the corner. Splashes water on her face. Looks at her hands a long time.*

*She lifts the chador from a hook on the door. Pulls it over her head. Adjusts it without a mirror.*

*She lights the samovar. Sits in her own chair. Looks at the other chair — empty.*

**MARYAM** *(voice, low, to no one)*
بلند شو مادر. چای دم آمد.
*(Get up, Mother. The tea is ready.)*

*She drinks alone. The light turns gold at the window.*

---

### SC 2 — EXT. OLD QUARTER ALLEY — MORNING

*A narrow alley. Adobe walls. A wooden door. Maryam closes it behind her and adjusts the chador under her chin.*

*She walks. The camera tracks beside her at a respectful distance.*

*On the wall: a pasted pamphlet — torn at one corner. We do not read what it says. She passes it without looking.*

*A CHILD runs past her with a paper kite. She watches him go.*

*A WOMAN'S VOICE from a window calls a name — not hers. Maryam keeps walking.*

*She passes a samovar shop. The shopkeeper nods. She nods back. Does not stop.*

CUT TO:

---

### SC 3 — EXT. BAZAAR-E BOZORG — MID-MORNING

*The bazaar. Vaulted brick ceilings. Shafts of sunlight from oculi above. Spice smell (suggested by the way she breathes in).*

*Maryam stops at a small bookseller's stall. Books stacked floor to ceiling. A beaded curtain hangs in the doorway of the back room.*

**MARYAM**
ببخشید. آقای رحیمی هستند؟
*(Excuse me. Is Mr. Rahimi here?)*

*A pause. From behind the curtain — a young man's voice. We never see him.*

**MAN'S VOICE (O.S.)**
پدرم فوت کرده، خانم. سه ماه پیش.
*(My father has passed, madam. Three months ago.)*

*Maryam absorbs this. Does not react.*

**MARYAM**
خدا رحمتش کند. من برای نامه‌ای آمده‌ام. از طرف یک دوست قدیمی. آقای رحیمی نگه داشته بود.
*(May God have mercy on him. I have come for a letter. From an old friend. Mr. Rahimi was keeping it.)*

*Silence. Then —*

**MAN'S VOICE (O.S.)**
نامه را به خانم فروزش داد. ایشان حالا در خانه‌ی قدیمی کنار چهل‌ستون زندگی می‌کنند. کوچه‌ی نسترن. درِ سبز.
*(He gave the letter to Mrs. Forouzesh. She now lives in the old house near Chehel Sotoun. Nastaran Alley. The green door.)*

**MARYAM**
ممنونم.
*(Thank you.)*

*She turns to leave. Stops. Looks back at the curtain.*

**MARYAM**
پدرتان مرد خوبی بود.
*(Your father was a good man.)*

*No answer. She walks on.*

CUT TO:

---

### SC 4 — EXT. SAFAVID COURTYARD — LATE AFTERNOON

*A Safavid-era courtyard. Pointed-arch tile portals. A turquoise dome visible above the wall. Two cypress trees. A stone bench. The light is amber, low, slanting from upper-left. Long shadows.*

*Maryam enters frame from the right, crosses the courtyard slowly. Deliberate step. Slight stoop at the shoulders.*

*She approaches the bench. On it: a small book — a volume of Hafez. Folded inside, the corner of an envelope.*

*She sits. Places her hand on the book. Does not open it yet.*

*A pigeon takes off from the dome. She follows it with her eyes.*

*She opens the book. Slides the letter out. The envelope is yellowed. Her name, in her father's hand: **مریم**.*

*She turns it over. Breaks the seal. Begins to read.*

CUT TO:

---

### SC 5 — EXT. COURTYARD / INT. MEMORY — CONTINUOUS

*Tight on the letter in her hands. Her father's handwriting, slanted, faded brown ink.*

**FATHER (V.O.)** *(warm, old, tired)*
مریمِ من. پدر آمد و نیامد.
*(My Maryam. Father came and did not come.)*

*FLASH IMAGE — over-exposed, grainy: a man's hand on a wooden door, hesitating, not knocking. We do not see his face.*

**FATHER (V.O.)**
سال سی‌ودو، یک روز پاییز، آمدم اصفهان. سرِ کوچه‌مان ایستادم. صدای گریه‌ی مادرت را شنیدم از پشت دیوار. برگشتم.
*(In '32, one autumn day, I came to Isfahan. I stood at the end of our alley. I heard your mother's weeping from behind the wall. I turned back.)*

*FLASH IMAGE: an alley wall. A woman's veiled silhouette through a curtained window. The man's hand falls to his side.*

**FATHER (V.O.)**
نخواستم درد را بیشتر کنم. حالا می‌فهمم که نبودنم بزرگ‌ترین درد بود.
*(I did not want to add to the pain. Now I understand that my absence was the greatest pain.)*

*Back to Maryam on the bench. She is very still. A single muscle moves in her jaw.*

**FATHER (V.O.)**
وطنی که برایش رفتم، هیچ‌گاه وجود نداشت. این را دیر فهمیدم. تو را هر روز دوست داشتم. این را زود فهمیدم و دیر گفتم.
*(The homeland I left for never existed. I understood this late. I loved you every day. This I understood early and said late.)*

*She folds the letter. Places it back in the book. Holds the book against her chest.*

*She looks up at the dome. The first crack of evening azan is heard, distant.*

CUT TO:

---

### SC 6 — EXT. ZAYANDEH-RUD RIVERBED — DUSK

*The dry bed of the Zayandeh-Rud. Stones. A few weeds. The Khaju Bridge in silhouette against a violet sky. The azan rolls across the city.*

*Maryam walks along the edge of the dry channel. She holds the book of Hafez under her chador.*

*She stops at the center of the bridge. Looks down. Where there should be water, there is dust.*

*She places one hand on the stone parapet.*

**MARYAM** *(quiet, to the riverbed)*
تو هم رفتی.
*(You too have gone.)*

*She does not weep. She walks on.*

CUT TO:

---

### SC 7 — INT. MARYAM'S ROOM — NIGHT

*The room is dark except for one oil lamp. Maryam sits on the floor by the bed. She has pulled out a small carved wooden box.*

*She opens it. Inside: three folded letters, yellowed. A photograph face-down. A pressed flower.*

*She takes the new letter from the book of Hafez. Looks at it once more. Folds it. Places it on top of the others.*

*Her hand rests on the closed box for a long moment.*

*She closes the lid. The brass clasp clicks.*

*She slides the box back under the bed.*

*She rises. Crosses to the window. Opens it. Cool night air.*

*She sits in her chair. The other chair is still empty. She does not speak to it.*

*She looks out the window. Holds the book of Hafez in her lap.*

FADE.

---

### SC 8 — INT. MARYAM'S ROOM / EXT. WINDOW — DAWN

*The room. Same angle as Scene 1. Blue pre-dawn light at the window.*

*The curtain moves slowly in the morning breeze.*

*The empty chair beside Maryam's chair.*

*Maryam's chair — also empty. The book of Hafez rests on the seat, closed.*

*Light at the window shifts from blue to gold.*

*Hold. Five seconds.*

*Slow fade to black.*
