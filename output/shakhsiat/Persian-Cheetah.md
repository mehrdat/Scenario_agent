# Character Bible — Persian Cheetah
## شخصیت‌ها — یوز ایرانی

> *Subjects, not characters.* This is a documentary essay film: the cheetahs are real, named animals. Their "arcs" are biographical, not dramaturgical. The bible's job is to make sure each animal lands as a **distinct emotional presence** on screen and is not lost in the species-level abstraction "the cheetah."

**Source:** `danesh/Persian-Cheetah-research.md`, `output/senaario/Persian-Cheetah/06-filmnaameh.md`.
**Style:** Kapadia / Attenborough — no human interview subjects foregrounded; the **animal is the protagonist of its own life.**
**Rule:** do not invent biographical facts about real animals or people. Anything marked `[verify]` must be cleared with ICS / DOE before broadcast.

---

## ENSEMBLE LOGIC — WHAT THE NAMED CATS ARE *FOR*

The film names six animals so the audience has somewhere for grief to land. The trap: a documentary that names a single dead animal (Pirouz) and rides his death for the third act becomes manipulative. The script avoids this by:

1. **Frontloading Kooshki** — so Pirouz is not the first death, only the most recent.
2. **Naming Delbar and Telma** — both living — so the program does not read as a story of dead animals.
3. **Naming the two unnamed cubs by their absence** (one syringe shot, one silent beat) — so the audience does not skip the casualty list.
4. **Refusing to name the wild cubs in Section 7** — anonymity is their safety; the film respects it.

**The math:** six named cheetahs + one named scientist + one named filmmaker = eight named beings, against a backdrop of unnamed wild animals and unnamed dead. The named carry the grief; the unnamed carry the hope.

## 1. KOOSHKI (کوشکی) — *The Captive King*

- **Species role:** Adult male Asiatic cheetah, ex-situ at Pardisan Park, Tehran. Captured as a cub in 2007 in Miandasht when his mother was killed by herders. Died 2018 of kidney failure, aged ~12. Father of Pirouz.
- **Visual silhouette:**
  - Slightly larger than expected for the subspecies. Coat slightly faded from indoor years — paler than wild kin.
  - Eyes the colour of weak tea. Tear-stripes prominent.
  - **Distinctive gait:** the *pacing rectangle* of the captive cat — eight steps, turn, eight steps. Whenever Kooshki is on screen and moving, he is pacing.
- **Silhouette test:** the only adult cheetah seen behind bars / mesh. Foreground geometry of enclosure = Kooshki.
- **Symbolic weight:** the **face of the species in Iranian memory**. Every Iranian over 20 knows him from school textbooks and IRIB nature segments. He is the audience's *introduction* to the yuz — the cat they think they remember.
- **Arc in the film:**
  - Section 5 (5:00–5:12). Introduced as the origin-story figure: the orphan cub, the herder rifle, the long captivity, the kidney-failure death.
  - His function is to **install the pattern** — orphaning, captivity, kidney failure — that will repeat in his son Pirouz. The audience does not consciously notice. The grammar of the film does.
- **Emotional function:** *recognition + low-grade guilt.* The audience sees a familiar face and remembers that they used to care, and then stopped.
- **Audio signature:** **silence.** Kooshki is shown only in archival photographs and one paced-enclosure video. No vocalisation. The cellos do not play for him.
- **The one shot:** the held photograph, head turned to camera, eyes meeting the lens. The film parks here for a beat of silence before moving to Delbar.

## 2. DELBAR (دلبر) — *The Unseen Mother*

- **Species role:** Adult female, captive-breeding program at Touran. The film names her as the female "who would not breed for years and then, against every expectation, did." `[verify exact role in 2022 litter — some accounts list Delbar as the secondary breeding female and Iran as the dam; confirm with ICS]`.
- **Visual silhouette:**
  - Sleeker, smaller-framed than Kooshki. Watchful posture. Rarely directly facing camera in archive.
  - Often photographed half-turned, mid-step, as if about to leave the frame.
- **Symbolic weight:** the *unfinished sentence*. Where Iran and Pirouz get news coverage, Delbar sits in the program file uncelebrated. She is the reminder that the program has more than one animal, that hope is technical and ongoing, not theatrical.
- **Arc in the film:**
  - Section 5 (5:12–5:24). One sentence. *"The one who took her time."*
  - Her job is to interrupt the dead-animals-only pattern that would otherwise dominate Section 5.
- **Emotional function:** *the not-yet-mourned.* She makes the audience aware that the story continues past the news cycle.
- **The one shot:** if archival exists — Delbar half-turned, eyes on the keeper, off-camera. Otherwise: a name on a clipboard, a feeding chart with her line of data.

## 3. IRAN (ایران) — *The First Mother*

- **Species role:** Adult female, captive-breeding program at Touran. **First Asiatic cheetah to give birth in captivity** — 1 May 2022, three cubs.
- **Visual silhouette:**
  - Lean, alert, *tired*. Post-partum thinness in the archive footage.
  - Always shown indoors — concrete pens, straw, fluorescent overhead.
  - The eyes are the giveaway: she watches the humans more than the cubs.
- **Silhouette test:** the only female cheetah shown *with cubs* (or referenced as their mother) in the entire film.
- **Symbolic weight:** the *nation as mother*. Her literal name — Iran — makes her the bearer of the national projection. The film does not over-press this. The naming is enough.
- **Arc in the film:**
  - Section 5 (5:24–5:36). She arrives, gives birth. Loses two cubs in fourteen days. Hands the survivor to a veterinarian. She does not appear again.
  - Her departure from the narrative is its own statement. She lived. The film moves on. So did the country.
- **Emotional function:** *the gap between effort and outcome.* She did the impossible thing. The impossible thing was not enough.
- **The one shot:** her face at week-two, watching the gloved hand take the surviving cub for bottle-feeding. No voiceover. Hold three seconds. *(Use only if archival exists with proper licensing; otherwise the name card carries the work.)*

## 4. THE TWO LOST CUBS — *The Unnamed*

- **Species role:** Two of Iran's three cubs. Died within fourteen days. Causes: one lung deformity, one unclear.
- **Visual silhouette:** wet, blind, palm-sized. Filmed once, briefly, in week one. **Never named.**
- **Symbolic weight:** the **non-event the film refuses to skip.** Most coverage of the 2022 birth jumped from "three cubs born" to "Pirouz, the survivor." The script names the two deaths in one sentence ("Two of the three did not see fourteen days") — and the bible argues this sentence carries more weight than its length suggests. The story of an endangered species is *mostly* the story of failed cubs.
- **Arc in the film:** Section 5, Scene 28a — one line of narration. One beat of held silence on the gloved hand with the syringe. The absence is the shot.
- **Emotional function:** *the casualty count the audience would otherwise forget.* The two losses prepare the emotional ground for the third loss (Pirouz) to land.
- **The one shot:** the gloved hand and syringe. No cub visible. The absence is the shot.

## 5. KOUSHKI II (کوشکی دوم) — *The Inheritor*

- **Species role:** Adult male, second-generation captive line. Named after his progenitor as a continuity marker in the breeding program.
- **Visual silhouette:** slightly smaller than the elder Kooshki. Coat fully sand-toned (less faded; he is younger and has more natural light exposure).
- **Symbolic weight:** **the continuity itself.** Where Kooshki the Elder is a memorial, Koushki II is the program's claim that the line continues. The repetition of the name across generations is the small, precise gesture of conservation hope.
- **Arc in the film:** Section 5 (5:36–5:48). One sentence. *"A male of the next generation."*
- **Emotional function:** *the rhyme.* The audience hears the same name twice in three minutes and registers — consciously or not — that the program is trying to repeat what worked.
- **The one shot:** a clear, slightly elevated portrait — eye-level, calm. The shot is *unfreighted*, contrast to the heavier Kooshki portrait.

---

## 6. TELMA (تلما) — *The Matriarch / The Map-Maker*

- **Species role:** Adult female. The matriarch of the early captive program. The collared female whose two-year GPS data drew the first true map of Asiatic cheetah land use.
- **Visual silhouette:** older posture; the collar visible in archival footage is her tell — a thick band of black against the sand coat.
- **Symbolic weight:** **the technology that worked.** Telma is the only named cheetah in the film whose contribution is data, not body. She is what conservation looks like when it succeeds methodologically — even if the species is failing demographically.
- **Arc in the film:** Section 5 (5:48–6:00). Two sentences. *"A collar around her neck, for two years, drew the first true map of how a Yuz uses her land. We learned more from Telma than from a century of hunters."*
- **Emotional function:** *the quiet achievement.* The audience exhales for one beat. The program has done something. Then the film returns to grief.
- **The one shot:** Telma in the field — collared, walking, not facing camera. The collar is the punctuation.

---

## 7. PIROUZ (پیروز) — *The Victorious*

- **Species role:** Surviving male cub of Iran's 2022 litter. Hand-raised. Imprinted on humans. Could not be released. **Died 28 February 2023 of kidney failure, at ten months.**
- **Visual silhouette across age stages — design these as a graded sequence:**
  - **Week 4:** palm-sized, eyes barely open, bottle-fed by a gloved hand. Vulnerable.
  - **Month 3:** kitten body, oversized paws, tear-stripes filling in. Wobbly.
  - **Month 6:** distinctly cheetah now — long-legged, batting a feather toy. *The image the country fell in love with.*
  - **Month 9:** lean, almost-adolescent, eyes weary. Last archival frames before the crisis.
  - **The aftermath:** never shown. The empty incubator replaces the body.
- **Silhouette test:** only cub-aged cheetah shown solo to camera in the entire film. Wherever you see a cub's face filling the frame, it is Pirouz.
- **Symbolic weight:** the **child everyone watched grow up and bury.** A whole nation's grief was projected on this ten-month-old animal. The script handles this with restraint — one cello note, one news ticker, one held incubator. The bible insists: *do not add more.* The restraint is the meaning.
- **Arc in the film:**
  - Section 5 (6:00–7:00). Birth → bottle-feeding → age montage → empty incubator → final living portrait.
  - Section 7 (end-card): "Pirouz (2022 — 2023)." His name closes the film.
- **Emotional function:** *the named loss.* The film argues — implicitly — that the audience cannot mourn "the species." They can only mourn a named animal. Pirouz is the named animal. To love him is to love what he was a member of.
- **Audio signature:** the **single sustained cello note** that enters at 6:30. It is *his*. It returns in Section 7 to bury him into the wider landscape.
- **The one shot:** the empty veterinary incubator under fluorescent light. Pirouz is, by this point, no longer in the film. He is only absence. The film knows this; the audience learns it in the holding.

## 8. THE WILD MOTHER + FIVE CUBS — *The Counter-Argument*

- **Species role:** Anonymous wild female photographed by ICS camera-trap in Touran, with five cubs, autumn 2023. *Not in captivity. Not in a clinic. On their own ground.*
- **Visual silhouette:**
  - Infrared / dusk camera-trap footage. Grainy. One adult in foreground, five small forms behind.
  - Movement: *single file along a ridge.* The mother glances back once.
  - This is the only **freely-moving** cheetah image in the entire film with multiple animals.
- **Silhouette test:** the only cheetahs shown **outside** human reach — no enclosure, no clinic, no road. The geometry of the shot is ridge and dusk, not pen and gate.
- **Symbolic weight:** the **counter-argument.** Every named cheetah in the film is dead or captive. This anonymous family is alive, free, and uncountable. The film's argument against despair sits on this one camera-trap clip.
- **Arc in the film:** Section 7 (9:00–9:20). One held sequence. Mother + five cubs walking. Mother glances back. Cut to title card.
- **Emotional function:** *the door left open.* If the film ended at Pirouz, it would be a eulogy. The wild mother keeps the verdict pending.
- **The one shot:** the mother's backward glance. Half a second. That is the film's argument: she still looks back.

## 9. KAVOUS SEYED-EMAMI (کاووس سیدامامی) — *The Witness Who Was Punished for Witnessing*

- **Real person.** Biologist, photographer, sociologist, founder of the Persian Wildlife Heritage Foundation (PWHF). Arrested by Iranian security services in January 2018 on espionage charges. Died in custody two weeks later (cause officially "suicide"; widely contested).
- **Treatment in the film:** archival photograph only. **No reconstruction. No editorialisation. No on-camera interview with anyone who knew him.** The film states the documented facts and lets the silence speak.
- **Visual handling:**
  - One public-domain or licensed news photograph.
  - Persian + English name card.
  - Held five seconds. No longer.
- **Symbolic weight:** **what conservation costs in Iran today.** The Western audience will hear "biologist died in custody" as abstract. The Iranian audience will hear a name they know. Both audiences need the line that follows: *"Their crime was a camera in a tree."* This is the bravest sentence in the film. Protect it.
- **Arc in the film:** Section 6 (7:00–7:18). One photograph. Two sentences of narration. Then the film moves on — because his work continues without him.
- **The one shot:** his portrait, looking at the photographer (whoever they were), in the field, before everything. **Do not editorialise the image. Let the photograph carry its own grief.**

## 10. MEHRDAD — *The Address*

- **Real person.** The filmmaker. Iranian. Speaks in his own voice in Section 7 only.
- **On-camera presence:**
  - Mid-shot, 50mm, soft daylight from window left.
  - Direct address — looking into the lens, not past it.
  - Room tone audible. No professional voice grading. Slightly closer mic than the narrator.
  - Posture: still, slightly forward, no performance.
- **Voice register:**
  - Slower than the BBC narrator. Conversational, not declarative.
  - First-person singular (*I*), then a single first-person plural that closes the section.
  - Five sentences. Six at most. The discipline of the film holds even here.
- **Symbolic weight:** **the address.** Everything before 8:30 has been omniscient. Mehrdad's arrival on screen is the moment the film stops being *about* and becomes *to*. The viewer is no longer a spectator.
- **Arc in the film:** Section 7 (8:30–9:00). Thirty seconds. Then the film hands itself to the wild mother and her cubs.
- **The one shot:** the moment his eyes find the lens. The film has waited 8 minutes 30 seconds for this gaze. Earn it by withholding everywhere else.

## RELATIONSHIP MATRIX

| Pair | Relationship | What the audience reads |
|---|---|---|
| Kooshki → Pirouz | Father (genetic line) | Inherited fragility. The pattern of orphaning and kidney failure across generations. The film does not state this — the rhyme is in the editing. |
| Iran → Pirouz | Biological mother who could not nurse | The handover from animal mother to human keeper. The moment the species enters human dependency. |
| Iran → the two lost cubs | Biological mother of the unnamed dead | The fourteen-day silence. The price of the impossible birth. |
| Kooshki ↔ Koushki II | Naming inheritance | The program's claim that the line continues. The single most quietly hopeful gesture in the film. |
| Kooshki ↔ Iran ↔ Delbar ↔ Telma | The captive triangle (square) | The breeding program as a small, closed world. Four faces, one programme. |
| Pirouz ↔ the wild mother's five cubs | Captive dead vs. wild alive | The two possible futures of the subspecies. The film refuses to choose. |
| Seyed-Emami ↔ the cameras still in the trees | Death of the witness vs. survival of the method | The crime continues; so does the work. |
| Mehrdad ↔ the audience | Address | The only relationship the film *makes* (as opposed to *documents*). |

## VOICE / NARRATION DESIGN

The narrator is the only sustained human voice in the film. Treat her as an unseen character:

- **Register:** informed elder, not boom-voice authority. Reference: BBC Attenborough cadence; Persian-side reference is Farhad Mehrad gravitas / IRIB *Mostanad* restraint.
- **Posture toward the animals:** never anthropomorphises. Never says "he was sad." Says the facts. Lets the audience supply the feeling.
- **Posture toward the audience:** assumes intelligence. Never explains the obvious. Never repeats a fact.
- **The first-person plural rule:** narration uses *we / us* **zero times** before Mehrdad's section. Mehrdad uses it **once**, near the end. *(Verify in 06-filmnaameh.md — currently confirmed.)*
- **Pause discipline:** narrator pauses are written into the script (e.g., the held beat before "Yuz"). The mix engineer must honour them. The pauses *are* the writing.

## DELIBERATE OMISSIONS

| What we do not show | Why |
|---|---|
| Pirouz's body or any image post-mortem | Restraint. The empty incubator is the body. |
| Reconstruction of Seyed-Emami's death | Ethical boundary. The film does not speculate. |
| Faces of game-guards or villagers | The Kapadia rule + the political risk to subjects. Silhouette only. |
| Sentimental music under Kooshki | He died in captivity in 2018. The film has not yet earned music. |
| The cubs' names in Section 7 | They have none. The film respects their anonymity as their safety. |

## DIRECTOR'S INSTRUCTION (load-bearing)

**Do not add more named animals.** The math is correct at six. The film carries six names so the audience can carry six names. A seventh would be a generosity the form cannot absorb. The seventh blank line in the final title card is the audience's responsibility, not the filmmaker's reservoir.
